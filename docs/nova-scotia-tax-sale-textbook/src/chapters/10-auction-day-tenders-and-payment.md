# Auction Day, Tenders and Payment

::: {.chain links="handoff"}
Research chain: notice → parcel → context → unknowns → **handoff**. This chapter follows the
finished file into the sale itself, where the bidder, the advisers and the municipality each act on
it.
:::

::: {.objectives}
1. Compare an open-outcry auction with a public tender, and explain why the written maximum enters
   both unchanged.
2. State who the Municipal Government Act prohibits from buying, and keep eligibility, authority
   and event compliance as three separate questions.
3. Prepare a payment sheet: the statutory payment forms, the event's accepted forms, the immediate
   amount and the three-business-day balance.
4. Trace what happens when there is no sufficient bid, no immediate payment, a missed balance, a
   failed tender payment or a schedule change.
:::

## Two formats, one decision {#sec-10-two-formats}

### The file at the door

::: {.case name="Cedar Street"}
Composite case — not a real property.

The fictional Cedar Street file has reached the door of the auction room. Its intended use is
written. The unresolved conditions are written. The bidder's authority, payment route and maximum
are written. None of those entries will be improved by a faster heartbeat.
:::

### The open-outcry auction

Inverness County's August 2026 terms, checked for this edition, place the sale in St. Peter's
Parish Hall in Port Hood. The municipality describes a public auction. A bidder appears in person,
follows the event's registration rules and competes while the treasurer calls the property.

That is an **open-outcry auction**: a live public process in which participants respond to the bids
called in the room until no higher acceptable bid is offered.

The format exposes pressure. A bidder can hear the next increment, see another card rise and feel
the silence that follows. The leading price changes in public. The parcel evidence does not.

CBRM supplies a more detailed dated example of the mechanics. Its July 2026 instructions require
government-issued photo identification for registration and issue numbered bidding cards. Those are
CBRM's event instructions, not unwritten rules for every Nova Scotia sale. Inverness, CBRM and any
other municipality can publish different operational details within the governing law. The current
municipal notice and terms must travel with the bidder.

### The public tender

Nova Scotia law also permits a different format. Outside Halifax, the Municipal Government Act makes
public auction the usual route but allows the treasurer, with council's consent, to call tenders
instead.

A **public tender** is a defined process in which bidders submit offers by the stated deadline and
the municipality opens and evaluates them under the event's terms. The competing offers are not
adjusted through live calls across a room.

Pictou County's April 2026 sale provides a dated sealed-tender example. A bidder could not watch the
competing amount rise and add one more increment. The amount had to be chosen before the tender left
the bidder's control, under that event's stated submission rules.

Annapolis County's 2026 records show why the format label needs an event date. Its public site
preserves results from a March tax sale and later results from a June sale by tender. That sequence
is evidence that a municipality may use more than one statutory route over time. It does not make
the closed June tender a current invitation, and it does not establish the rules for the next
Annapolis event.

### Opposite temptations, one maximum

Open bidding and tendering create opposite temptations:

- In the room, the bidder may chase a visible rival.
- At a desk, the bidder may raise a sealed offer to avoid losing by an imagined small difference.

Neither temptation adds title, access, condition, possession, tax or use evidence. The Cedar Street
maximum therefore enters either format unchanged ([Figure 10.1](#fig-10-auction-versus-tender)).

For an open auction, Maya's rehearsal is physical and short. She knows who is authorized to bid,
which card represents that authority and the exact amount at which the card stays down.

For a tender, the same authority is expressed through the event's stated submission rules and
deadline. The ceiling is checked before submission. There is no second private number called "what
I would have bid if I knew."
:::

### Who may not buy

Authority includes more than possessing identification or a signed letter. The Municipal Government
Act prohibits certain people from buying land at the selling municipality's tax sale, directly or
through an agent. The prohibited group includes:

- a council member or employee of that municipality;
- specified village commission members or employees;
- their spouses;
- a company in which one of those people owns or beneficially owns a majority of the outstanding
  shares.

The statute attaches a penalty and possible loss of office or employment.

That conflict rule is narrower than a complete eligibility review. It does not answer the federal,
tax, corporate-authority or representation questions from [Chapter 9](#sec-09-build-backward). Nor does successful event
registration certify that every purchase rule has been satisfied. It identifies one statutory group
that cannot solve the problem by bidding through somebody else.

### Three questions on the pre-event sheet

Maya's pre-event sheet can now separate three questions that are often blurred. A yes in one box
does not fill the other two.

| Question | What it asks |
|---|---|
| Eligibility | Is she legally eligible to acquire this kind of property? |
| Authority | Does she or her agent have documented authority to make this particular bid? |
| Event compliance | Has she complied with the event's registration or tender-submission instructions? |

The researcher who assembled Cedar Street's evidence file does not answer those questions for her.
The file supplies sources, dates, boundaries and unresolved professional questions. Maya and the
appropriate advisers establish her authority. The municipality administers its event. That division
keeps careful research from turning into an unauthorized purchase decision.

### One durable rule

The two formats now collapse into one durable rule. A bidding card and a tender envelope may feel
entirely different, but each carries the same evidence-bound maximum, the same conflict prohibition
and the same requirement for lawful authority. Procedure changes how an offer is delivered. It does
not enlarge what the file supports.

## The finish line beyond the hammer {#sec-10-finish-line}

### Refresh the source on the morning

Auction morning begins with a source refresh, not with the first bid. The August Inverness packet is
a dated list of 45 liens. Its own summary and detail pages require reconciliation. More important on
the day, any listed account may have been paid, removed or otherwise changed before its turn
arrives.

The live municipal status controls. A saved PDF, an NS Marks screenshot and a research worksheet
preserve what was known at their recorded times. They do not keep a parcel in the sale. If Cedar
Street is withdrawn, Maya's authority to bid ends with that announcement. Research about the parcel
cannot convert a withdrawn lot into a private offer.

### When the property is called

If the property is called, the worksheet changes jobs. It no longer helps Maya discover a price. It
tells her what she already authorized. Each bid below the maximum remains possible. A bid above it
remains prohibited by her own file.

The open-outcry pressure can be rehearsed with one short sequence:

1. The called amount is below Maya's ceiling. Her card rises.
2. Another card raises the price.
3. She responds while the amount remains within authority.
4. The next call crosses the line. Her card stays down.

::: {.recall}
This is the Chapter 9 test, applied under noise.

1. Did the higher call establish a new right of access, improve title, empty the building, lower
   the tax branch or put money in the bank? It did none of those things. Another participant offered
   more.
2. Did Maya's authority change? No. Maya's research did not change, so her authority did not
   change.
:::

### Payment forms: the statute and the event

The payment path needs the same advance rehearsal. Under the Municipal Government Act, statutory
payment forms include:

- cash;
- certified cheque;
- money order;
- bank draft;
- irrevocable letter of credit;
- a lawyer's trust cheque.

Council may authorize another legal-tender delivery method. That permission does not require a
particular event to accept every possible method. The current sale terms decide what the
municipality will take and how it must be delivered.

Inverness's August 2026 terms list cash, certified cheque, money order, debit, bank draft and
lawyer's trust cheque. Its FAQ says the successful bidder must immediately pay the taxes, interest
and expenses for which the property was sold, plus the stated $200 deed-registration amount. Any
remaining purchase price is due within three business days.

### The deposit

The **deposit** is the immediate amount paid toward the purchase before a remaining balance is
completed. In the statutory auction structure, the buyer must immediately pay the whole purchase
price or a smaller amount equal to the taxes, interest and expenses for which the land was sold.

Event-specific fees or payment instructions can sit beside that obligation. A bidder should use the
municipality's current terminology and amount rather than treating "deposit" as a universal flat
fee.

### Two readiness tests

This creates two separate readiness tests:

1. The immediate amount must be available in an accepted form when the sale requires it.
2. The rest must be available through a verified path within three business days.

A future plan to ask a lender is not the same as ready funds. A bank draft for one amount is not
automatically adjustable in the room, and a debit card is useful only if the municipality accepts it
and the financial institution permits the transaction.

CBRM's July 2026 instructions make the operational point concrete: the immediate payment and
three-business-day balance are part of the event mechanics. The durable lesson is that payment
constraints belong in the bid file, not in a set of errands created after the hammer.

::: {.case name="Cedar Street · Maya's payment sheet"}
Composite case — not a real property; Maya is a fictional bidder.

Maya therefore carries a payment sheet for Cedar Street. It identifies:

- the current event;
- the amount required immediately;
- the accepted method in hand;
- the account or instrument that can complete the balance;
- the three-business-day deadline;
- the person authorized to act.

If one required field is unverified, the parcel is not ready even when the maximum appears
affordable.
:::

### Tenders move the clock

Tendering moves the moment but preserves the test. Pictou's dated April 2026 notice required the
successful tenderer to pay the bid price in full within three days after notification. There was no
live hammer followed by an immediate auction deposit under those tender terms. The bidder still
needed the accepted payment route before submitting. Format changed the clock's starting event; it
did not create ordinary financing conditions after acceptance.

::: {.rail label="A way to picture it"}
The finish-line analogy helps as long as it remains limited. The hammer or accepted tender
identifies the leading offer. Payment performance lies beyond that marker.

The limit: real legal completion also includes the later certificate, possible redemption and deed
stages, so this is not a complete race from bid to ownership. It only prevents the leading price
from being mistaken for a finished sale.
:::

### When payment, not the price, fails

::: {.case name="A different fictional parcel"}
Composite case — not a real property; Maya is a fictional bidder.

Suppose Maya is the high bidder on a different fictional parcel within her maximum, but the bank
draft she expected cannot be issued that day. The evidence file may be sound and the price may be
authorized. Her payment performance is not. Raising the maximum would solve nothing. Asking the sale
to wait would attempt to change the process after the bid.
:::

The disciplined outcome is harsher and clearer. Ready means:

1. eligible authority;
2. current event status;
3. an intact ceiling;
4. an accepted immediate payment;
5. a verified balance path.

When those conditions hold, the bid can be executed. When any one fails, keeping the card down is
the completed decision.

## When the expected sale does not happen {#sec-10-breaks}

### The straight line and its branches

The ordinary event story runs in a straight line: the property is called, a highest acceptable bid
emerges, payment is made and the treasurer proceeds toward a certificate of sale. The statute also
contains branches for the times when that line breaks. [Figure 10.3](#fig-10-sale-state-changes)
draws them together.

### First break: no sufficient bid

The first break occurs before there is a purchaser. Suppose the recovery amount is called and no
participant offers enough. Under the Municipal Government Act, the treasurer may bid the taxes,
interest and expenses and purchase the land for the municipality. The Act also permits a
municipality to bid and buy through an official or agent for a municipal purpose.

"May" matters. No sufficient private bid does not force the municipality to acquire every parcel.
If the municipality does not purchase, it may advertise the property again and later sell it at
auction for the best obtainable price or by highest tender, subject to any acceptable minimum
directed by council. The process remains municipal and advertised.

An observer cannot fill that pause with a private proposal. "Unsold" does not mean available at the
recovery amount, available over the counter or available on conditions selected by the observer.
CBRM's current result legend (as checked in July 2026) states the operational boundary directly: an
unsold property may be purchased only at a future tax sale. Another municipality's next step must be
checked in its own current record.

### Second break: no immediate payment

The second break occurs immediately after a high bid. The auction statute requires the purchaser to
pay the price or the required smaller amount at once. If the purchaser does not, the treasurer must
put the land up for sale again forthwith. The apparent winner drops out before the event can move
through the payment step.

CBRM's current instructions (July 2026) translate that branch into room language. A bid is void if
immediate payment cannot be made, and the property is auctioned again. The next person does not
automatically inherit the first winning amount. The treasurer reopens the process under the event's
rules.

::: {.case name="Cedar Street · a failed bid in the room"}
Composite case — not a real property; Maya is a fictional bidder.

Maya, if she is still eligible and prepared, returns to her original ceiling. Another person's
failed bank card does not improve Cedar Street or enlarge her authority.
:::

### Third break: the balance is missed

The third break occurs after an immediate deposit has been made but the balance does not arrive
within three business days. The Act sends the land back to advertisement and sale. Resale expenses
are deducted from the deposit, and the remaining balance of the deposit is refunded after the resale
is held. The amount and timing of that result depend on the actual municipal process; the book
cannot calculate them for a bidder in advance.

This later failure carries a longer shadow than the first one. The municipality must run another
sale path. The bidder's funds remain tied to a future event, subject to the statutory expense
deduction. A maximum that was affordable only if financing could be arranged afterward was never
operationally affordable.

### Tender failure

Tender failure follows its governing route as well. The statute gives the person whose tender is
accepted three business days after notification to pay the tender price. Failure sends the land back
toward advertisement and sale, with the statutory treatment of resale expenses and any deposit.

A municipal tender notice may also describe how acceptance, rejection or movement to another bid is
administered. The current Act, council authority and exact event terms must be read together rather
than blended from different municipalities.

### State changes, not opportunities

These outcomes can sound like opportunities to someone who studied the parcel and missed the first
call. They are better understood as state changes:

- Before the event, the parcel is advertised.
- During the event, it may be withdrawn, receive no sufficient bid or produce a leading offer.
- After a leading offer, it may fail immediate payment or later balance payment.

Each change points to a specific municipal action and a fresh source check.

### Schedule changes

A schedule change requires the same discipline. The statutory notice states an advertised date, time
and place. The reviewed tax-sale provisions do not supply a general bidder-facing script for every
postponement, cancellation or rescheduling dispute. An old advertisement therefore cannot prove that
a changed event is proceeding, cancelled or validly rescheduled.

::: {.case name="Cedar Street · a changed date"}
Composite case — not a real property; Maya is a fictional bidder.

Suppose heavy weather closes the hall, or the municipality posts a revised date. Maya does not carry
her old calendar entry forward as authority. She returns to the municipal page and current notice,
confirms the affected properties, time, place, registration and payment terms, and seeks legal
advice if the change itself is disputed. A social-media post or another bidder's memory may point
her toward the source. It does not replace it.
:::

### When the parcel disappears

The same refresh applies when Cedar Street disappears. The research file keeps its dated records
because they explain what was known and why Maya was once prepared. Its status field changes. The
file cannot declare whether the owner paid, the municipality deferred the parcel or another legal
event occurred unless the authoritative source establishes that fact.

### A completed payment starts a new clock

A completed payment path starts a new clock. Cedar Street is in the redeemable branch. If Maya stays
within her authority, satisfies the event terms and pays the purchase money for that parcel, the
treasurer gives a certificate of sale. In this branch, that certificate is the next legal stage, not
a receipt for instant possession or unrestricted ownership (Chapter 11).

If the sale does not reach that point, the outcome still has a disciplined description:

- withdrawn;
- no sufficient bidder;
- municipal purchase;
- immediate re-offer;
- later re-advertisement;
- verified event change.

None authorizes a guess. None raises the written maximum. Every branch returns the reader to the same
reliable place: the current municipal source and the limits of the evidence file.
