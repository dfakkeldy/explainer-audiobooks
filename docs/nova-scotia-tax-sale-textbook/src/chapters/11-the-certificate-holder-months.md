# The Certificate-Holder Months

::: {.chain links="handoff"}
Research chain: notice → parcel → context → unknowns → **handoff**. In the certificate-holder
months, each unsettled question about entry, insurance, repairs, income and redemption goes to the
municipality, counsel, an insurer or a qualified professional before anything physical happens.
:::

::: {.objectives}
1. Describe the certificate holder's operating lane: protect the land, collect rent, use it without
   diminishing its value, do not cut trees or injure the premises, and insure insurable buildings.
2. Keep the first operating file and handle new tax bills.
3. Explain insurable interest, and why one insurer's refusal is evidence rather than a legal
   finding.
4. Follow a necessary repair through the treasurer's written approval.
5. Name the redemption formula's categories and offsets, and the moment the purchaser's rights end.
:::

::: {.keyterms}
- insurable interest
- necessary repairs
:::

## Responsibility before certainty {#sec-11-responsibility}

### A middle stage begins

::: {.case name="Maya and Cedar Street"}
Composite case — not a real property; Maya is a fictional bidder.

Maya leaves the fictional Cedar Street sale with complete payment behind her. The treasurer gives
her a certificate of sale describing the land and the purchase price, registers a copy, and serves
the statutory copies and redemption notice. The auction room is finished. A calendar has begun.

Cedar Street is redeemable. Maya does not yet have the tax deed that may follow after six months if
nobody redeems. She also does not receive vacant possession from the municipality merely because the
purchase money was paid.
:::

The certificate creates a real middle stage with real responsibilities, conditional rights and an
ending that may return the property to someone else.

### The certificate holder's operating lane

Nova Scotia's Municipal Government Act gives a certificate holder the owner-like powers needed to
protect the land. The same section sets out powers, limits and one duty:

- The holder may collect rents due or becoming due.
- The holder may use the land without diminishing its value.
- The section forbids cutting trees, injuring the premises, or knowingly allowing another person to
  do so.
- It also requires the purchaser to insure buildings if they are insurable.

Those sentences define a narrow operating lane. A leaking opening may need protection from the next
rain. A full redesign for Maya's proposed rehabilitation can wait. Removing mature trees for a future
view is outside the lane, and so is damage disguised as cleanup. The certificate period carries the
property through uncertainty; it is not an early construction season.

::: {.rail label="The idea"}
The difference can be tested with purpose and scale. Work aimed at arresting an immediate loss is
protective. Work aimed at changing the property for a future use is development.
:::

That distinction does not authorize either action by itself. Entry, occupancy, tenancy, safety,
permits, contractor authority and the exact condition still matter. When any of those questions is
unsettled, the next move belongs with the municipality, lawyer, insurer or appropriate professional,
not with a crowbar and a private interpretation of the statute.

### Possession and safety

Cedar Street appears vacant from public-road observations. The word "appears" stays in the file.
Curtains, vehicles, utilities, stored goods or a quiet building cannot prove lawful vacancy.

::: careful
Maya does not force entry, change locks, confront a person or direct somebody to remove belongings.
She asks counsel how the certificate power interacts with the actual possession facts and records the
answer before any physical step.
:::

Human safety runs beside legal authority. A damaged roof, unstable porch, exposed wiring, mould,
fuel, animals or an unknown occupant can turn a seemingly minor check into an emergency. The
certificate does not make Maya a building inspector, electrician, environmental professional or
peace officer. An urgent threat is routed to the appropriate emergency or municipal authority.
Non-urgent condition work is scoped by qualified people using lawful access.

### The first operating file

The first operating file therefore begins with the registered certificate and the sale date. Beside
them go:

- the redemption deadline;
- the municipal contact;
- property-tax account instructions;
- possession advice;
- insurer communications;
- photographs or reports lawfully obtained;
- approvals, invoices and receipts;
- rent records and other income records.

Every item carries a date and source. The file must be able to explain what Maya knew, what authority
she relied on, what she did and what money moved. [Figure 11.1](#fig-11-certificate-holder-calendar)

### New tax bills

The new tax bills matter immediately. The redemption formula can include taxes levied after the sale
and related interest. That statutory possibility is not a reason to ignore the bills and expect
another person to absorb them later.

Maya confirms with the municipality where notices will go, how the account is tracked during the
certificate period and what must be paid when due. A missed notice can create a practical problem
even when the statute later recognizes a category of tax.

### Insurance: interest, vacancy and the inquiry

Insurance begins with a similar distinction. The Act says Maya is deemed to have an **insurable
interest** in the land: a legally recognized stake that can support seeking insurance against a
loss. It answers why Maya may insure during this conditional stage. It does not command an insurance
company to accept Cedar Street, define the coverage, or make the premium affordable.

Vacancy changes the underwriting conversation. Current Canadian insurer materials (checked July 2026)
describe vacant buildings as presenting risks that ordinary home coverage may not continue to handle,
and explain that special vacancy or non-standard property underwriting may be required. Those
materials illustrate market practice. They do not prove that Maya, this legal interest or this
building will qualify for any advertised product.

::: {.case name="Cedar Street · the insurance inquiry"}
Composite case — not a real property; Maya is a fictional bidder.

Maya's inquiry therefore states the awkward facts rather than smoothing them away. She identifies:

- the tax-sale certificate stage;
- the redemption period;
- the current possession information;
- the known building condition;
- intended protective measures;
- any access limits.

She asks whether the insurer will cover her exact interest, which risks are included or excluded,
what inspections or safeguards are required, when coverage would begin and how a redemption or later
deed would affect it.

The first underwriter declines. A specialty broker later identifies a possible form of coverage
subject to conditions. Maya obtains the actual terms and professional advice before treating that
response as coverage.
:::

The refusal is useful evidence. It is dated, kept with the questions asked and routed back into
Maya's cost and risk file. It does not prove that the building is uninsurable in the statutory sense,
because one company's underwriting decision is not a legal determination for every market. It also
does not permit Maya to declare the duty impossible and stop looking without advice.

Each stage of an insurance answer is a different record:

| The record | What it is not |
|---|---|
| A product webpage | It is not a quote. |
| A quote | It is not a bound policy. |
| A policy effective on a stated date | It is not proof that every peril or activity is covered. |

The operating file preserves each stage instead of replacing it with the convenient word "insured."

### The approved repair

::: {.case name="Cedar Street · the loose roof covering"}
Composite case — not a real property; Maya is a fictional bidder.

While the insurance work continues, a qualified exterior assessment identifies a loose roof covering
that could admit water during the next storm. This is the moment when "protect the land" can become
dangerously broad.

Maya does not order a replacement roof and hope to add the bill to redemption. She sends the
treasurer a defined scope, supporting assessment and cost, asks for the required written approval,
and checks the other legal, safety, insurance and possession conditions before work begins.
:::

The statute treats reimbursement for repairs narrowly. The redemption amount may include amounts the
purchaser paid for **necessary repairs** to buildings when those repairs were made with the
treasurer's written approval. A necessary repair in this chapter is protective work supported by the
actual condition and the statutory approval path. The phrase is not a blank category for
improvements, preferred materials, project acceleration or work authorized only after the invoice
arrived.

Suppose the treasurer gives written approval for a temporary weatherproofing scope. The contractor
performs only that scope through a lawful and safe access arrangement. Maya keeps the approval,
report, invoice, proof of payment and dated completion record together. If the contractor discovers a
larger defect, work does not silently expand. The new condition returns through the same authority,
professional and insurance channels.

The approved repair receipt becomes the concrete centre of the file. It connects an observed risk to
a professional scope, a written municipal approval, a lawful performance route and an amount actually
paid ([Figure 11.2](#fig-11-approved-repair-chain)). Remove any one of those links and the
reimbursement claim becomes a different question. Good intentions cannot reconstruct missing
authority after redemption begins.

### Rent and other income

Rent and other income require equal discipline. The certificate holder may collect rent, but the
existence of a payment does not answer:

- who occupies the building;
- which tenancy or contract applies;
- who should be contacted;
- what lawful possession steps are available.

Maya routes those questions through counsel and the applicable records. Any income actually received
is logged by date and amount because it can reduce what is repaid on redemption.

### Active restraint

The result is active restraint.

| Maya does | Maya does not |
|---|---|
| preserve the registered certificate | renovate |
| handle current tax notices | harvest |
| disclose the true risk to insurers | take possession by force |
| document the refusal and any accepted coverage | treat six months as guaranteed ownership |
| protect against a defined loss through lawful authority | |
| account for income | |

Responsibility has arrived. Certainty has not.

## Closing the ledger when the property is redeemed {#sec-11-redemption}

### Month four

::: {.case name="Cedar Street · notice of redemption"}
Composite case — not a real property; Maya is a fictional bidder.

In month four, the Cedar Street file changes again. The treasurer tells Maya that a person with a
qualifying interest intends to redeem. The message does not erase the work already done, and it does
not begin a private negotiation between Maya and the person redeeming. It activates the statutory
close-out.
:::

Redemption is one of the outcomes built into a redeemable tax sale. The owner, a mortgage or lien
holder, a person with another charge, or another person with an interest in the land may use the
statutory route within the applicable six-month period. Maya bought with that possibility already in
the process. The return of the property through redemption is therefore not evidence that her
research failed.

### The people involved

The human scene deserves the same discipline as the financial one. A former owner may be trying to
preserve a home, family land or a business. An occupant may not be the person arranging payment.
Maya does not need their private story to close her certificate file.

Communication stays lawful, necessary and respectful, and the treasurer and counsel remain the route
for process questions. No one is threatened with entry, removal or construction work while the legal
stage is changing.

### The redemption formula

The amount paid to redeem is a statutory calculation, not a number Maya chooses. Under the Municipal
Government Act, the starting category is the sum the purchaser paid. Interest at 10 percent per year
runs on the total paid by the purchaser from the sale date to the redemption date. The formula can
also include:

- certain older unpaid taxes when the purchase payment did not cover them;
- taxes levied after the sale and related interest;
- the fee to record the discharge;
- qualifying fire-insurance premiums;
- necessary-repair amounts paid with the treasurer's written approval.

That list has offsets. Any balance remaining in the tax-sale surplus account for the property is
deducted, as is rent or other income earned by the purchaser from the land. The formula recognizes
supported carrying items while preventing the certificate holder from receiving both property income
and repayment as though the income never arrived ([Figure 11.3](#fig-11-redemption-ledger)).

::: {.rail label="Why it matters"}
The 10 percent line can dominate a bidder's memory because it sounds like a return. Its legal job
here is narrower: it is one component of the redemption formula for the actual period between sale
and redemption.
:::

The statute does not promise that redemption will occur, that the property will remain free of
loss, that every expense will qualify, or that the purchaser will avoid taxes, financing costs,
professional fees, uninsured damage and time. The 10 percent line is not a substitute for Chapter 9's
all-in analysis.

### The treasurer's calculation and Maya's records

No personalized arithmetic is needed to operate Maya's file. The treasurer determines the amount
required through the statutory process. Maya supplies the records for the categories that depend on
her conduct.

The concrete example is the approved weatherproofing receipt: written approval, exact scope, invoice and
proof of payment. Beside it are the policy documents and premium records. A folder of estimates,
verbal approvals and work Maya hoped to do is not the same ledger.

Each category has its own source and treatment:

| Category | Where it stays |
|---|---|
| The new tax bill | in the municipal account trail |
| The fire-insurance premium | in the purchaser's statement |
| The repair | linked to written approval |
| Rent and other income | in the offset column, even if they have already been spent |

Combining everything into one number too early would hide the distinctions the treasurer needs to
administer the redemption.

### The purchaser's statement

When redemption is to take place, the purchaser may be asked for a statement of amounts spent on
fire-insurance premiums and on repairs made with the treasurer's written approval. A complete
operating file makes that request ordinary. Reconstructing months of receipts, emails and income
after the request arrives makes it needlessly difficult.

Maya's statement is factual. It does not round a declined insurance quote into a premium, convert an
unpaid estimate into a repair, or include a broader future roof project. It lists amounts actually
spent in the supported categories and attaches the relevant evidence. Any disputed payment or
category belongs with the treasurer's official process and fact-specific legal advice, not a
certificate holder's improvised collection method.

### Redemption amount and purchaser repayment

The total redemption amount and the amount repaid to the purchaser should not be described as though
they are necessarily the same line-by-line list. The statute's redemption calculation includes
municipal tax and recording categories. Its purchaser-repayment section identifies the purchase sum,
interest, fire-insurance premiums and approved repairs, less rent or other property income.

The treasurer administers the whole transaction. Maya reconciles the payment she receives to the
official statement instead of inventing a private payoff figure.

### Halifax: a bounded example of sale expenses

Halifax makes the idea of sale expenses unusually visible, but only as a Halifax example. Halifax
operates under the parallel Halifax Regional Municipality Charter. The July 2026 snapshot of its
Administrative Order 18 schedule names local categories for notice, title searches, survey, deed
work, registration, closing and redemption administration.

That schedule teaches two bounded lessons:

1. "Sale expenses" can represent several real administrative and professional steps rather than one
   mysterious fee.
2. The governing municipality's current authority must supply the actual categories and amounts.

Halifax's categories are not a Nova Scotia tariff, do not describe an Inverness redemption, and can
change after this edition is drafted.

::: {.case name="Cedar Street · the local calculation"}
Composite case — not a real property; Maya is a fictional bidder.

Cedar Street's municipality may use different council-approved fees, forms and internal procedures.
Maya asks for the official calculation and current local authority. She does not copy a Halifax
category or amount into her worksheet. The comparison improves the question; it does not answer
another municipality's account.
:::

### The moment rights end

The close-out has a precise legal moment. A statement that someone plans to redeem does not end
Maya's interest, and delivery of part of the amount does not end it either. From the time the full
redemption amount is paid to the treasurer, the Act says the purchaser ceases to have a right to the
land.

The operating file records that municipal confirmation and stops any planned protective action that
no longer has a supporting interest. The documentary record also changes. Maya obtains and preserves
the municipality's official close-out record rather than treating a phone call or partial payment as
proof that the certificate stage has ended.

Insurance, contractors and anyone else relying on Maya's former certificate authority need prompt,
accurate notice through the appropriate channels:

- A policy may have its own cancellation or change terms.
- An approved contractor may need a stop instruction.
- Keys, documents or site arrangements, if any were lawfully created, need advice-led handling.

The discharge does not authorize Maya to settle those questions by holding property, demanding extra
money or entering the premises after her rights cease.

### The final ledger

Maya's final ledger preserves:

- the purchase record;
- the insurer refusal, the actual policy and the premium;
- tax notices;
- the written repair approval, invoice and payment receipt;
- the income record;
- the requested purchaser statement;
- the official redemption calculation and the purchaser repayment;
- the municipal close-out record.

It also preserves the human boundary: no private confrontation and no portrayal of redemption as
defeat.

::: {.case name="Cedar Street · the outcome"}
Composite case — not a real property; Maya is a fictional bidder.

The outcome is financially finite and legally meaningful. Maya receives the repayment the statute and
evidence support. The person redeeming keeps the land through the statutory process. Cedar Street
never reaches Maya's rehabilitation plan, yet her certificate-holder work can still be complete. She
protected what she was authorized to protect, documented what the formula could recognize and
stopped when the registered stage ended.
:::

If no redemption had occurred, the file would face a different transition after the statutory wait.
That path begins with a request for the deed, then reopens title, possession, condition and
intended-use questions under a new document (Chapter 12). The discharge closes this branch because a
lawful ending is not an interruption of the process. It is the process working.
