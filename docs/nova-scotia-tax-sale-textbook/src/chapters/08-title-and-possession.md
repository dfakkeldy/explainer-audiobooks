# Title and Possession

::: {.chain links="unknowns,handoff"}
Research chain: notice → parcel → context → **unknowns** → **handoff**. This chapter follows the
unknowns that remain after a deed, and hands each one to the authority that can answer it.
:::

   money route.

## What the deed actually changes {#sec-08-deed}

Before the book returns to pre-bid valuation (Chapter 9), this chapter deliberately steps forward to
a deeded case. The flash-forward isolates one question a bidder must understand in advance: what a
deed would change, and what it would leave for later.

::: {.case name="Foundry Street"}
Composite case — not a real property.

The fictional Foundry Street property's redemption question has been resolved, the municipal fee
has been paid, and a tax deed has been registered. A search of the correct PID now has a new
document at its centre.

Foundry Street's older parcel register shows a mortgage, a judgment, and a registered right-of-way
serving the house behind it.
:::

The deed matters. It changes the legal relationship to the land. It does not answer every question
that shares the property's address.

### Fee simple

Under Nova Scotia's Municipal Government Act, the tax deed is evidence that the statutory sale
provisions were followed. The Act says the deed vests the land in the grantee in **fee simple**.
Fee simple is the broad form of private ownership estate ordinarily meant when people speak of
owning land.

The phrase describes the legal interest conveyed. It does not describe the building's condition,
identify every person inside, certify a development plan, or turn a graphical parcel boundary into
a survey.

### Free and discharged from encumbrances

The Act also says the land is vested free and discharged from all **encumbrances**. An encumbrance
is a claim, interest, charge, or burden that can affect title to land. That statutory language is
powerful. It has a title job, and the exact job must be read with the rest of the statute, any
court order, the deed, and the property record.

A hurried summary of Foundry Street's register might say that the tax deed erased everything. The
statute itself prevents that summary:

- When an easement or right-of-way benefits the land being sold, the benefit passes with the land.
- When the land being sold is burdened by an easement or right-of-way, the sale does not terminate
  or affect that continuing relationship as the Act specifies.

### One right-of-way, two pieces of land

The right-of-way retrieves the access lesson from Chapter 6 in a fresh direction. There, the
question was whether a route gave the tax-sale parcel a legally supportable way out. Here, the
Foundry Street parcel may be the route another parcel relies on. The same registered relationship
can benefit one piece of land and burden another. A deed does not create a private island around
whichever PID changed hands most recently.

The title file therefore needs more than the phrase "free of encumbrances." It needs:

- the exact deed;
- the current parcel register;
- the instruments behind relevant entries;
- the legal description;
- a lawyer's interpretation of how the governing provisions apply to this parcel.

If a right-of-way continues, the file also needs its route, purpose, width, parties, maintenance
language, and any other terms the source instruments actually contain. None should be reconstructed
from a driveway line in imagery.

![**Three questions, not three synonyms.** Title, encumbrances and possession are related, but none is a synonym for the others: title asks what ownership interest is recorded, rights and burdens ask what easements, liens or continuing interests may matter, and possession asks who or what is actually on the land. ### The court-order branch

There can also be a court-order branch. A municipal treasurer may apply to a court for findings
about tax arrears and for an order addressing:

- the interests the deed will convey;
- the interests it will remain subject to;
- notice;
- the manner of sale.

A sale conducted under that route takes its legal effect from the statute and the actual order,
including any exceptions, exclusions, or partial interests set out there.

That makes the order part of the Foundry Street question, if an order exists. A note reading "court
approved" would treat two words as though they resolved the parcel. The useful note is:

::: {.filenote case="Foundry Street · court-order check"}
Confirm whether this sale proceeded under a court order. If so, obtain the order and have counsel
identify the conveyed interest, every stated exception or exclusion, the redemption treatment, and
the relationship between the order, deed, parcel register, and source instruments.
:::

### Surplus money follows a separate route

One money question must be kept outside this title analysis. A prior interest holder may have a
separate statutory route to claim a share of tax-sale surplus proceeds. That possible proceeds claim
is not automatically a continuing burden on the land. Chapter 12 follows the money route; this
chapter stays with title and possession.

### Why "clear title" is an unsafe label

This is why "clear title" is an unsafe all-purpose label. A municipality may describe the statutory
deed process, yet current municipal guidance also warns bidders that clear title is not guaranteed.
The two statements are not enemies; they do different work:

| Statement | What it does |
|---|---|
| The municipality's description of the statutory deed process | Describes the legal effect the statute gives a qualifying deed |
| The warning that clear title is not guaranteed | Refuses to promise that a municipality's auction page has completed a property-specific title opinion, found every factual problem, resolved possession, or made the interest readily insurable or marketable for a particular transaction |

Title quality can require questions beyond a list of visible register entries:

- Which statute governs this municipality?
- Was there a court order? What exactly did it say?
- Does the legal description match the land the bidder thought was being researched?
- Which easements or rights-of-way benefit or burden the land?
- Do the documents reveal an exception, exclusion, partial interest, boundary issue, or other
  qualification?
- Is another statute or property-specific record relevant?

Those questions belong with a Nova Scotia property lawyer reviewing the actual file. The book cannot
settle them by naming categories that might or might not apply.

::: {.rail label="The idea"}
The careful explanation preserves the deed's force without turning it into a magic eraser. For
Foundry Street:

- Its tax deed may discharge interests the statute discharges.
- Its registered right-of-way may continue.
- A court order may define the interest or preserve an exception.
- A former interest holder may have a route to surplus money rather than a surviving claim against
  the parcel.

Each result comes from its governing source.
:::

The answer should never depend on the reassuring sound of "fee simple" or "free of encumbrances"
alone. Those phrases have legal content. Their safe use is specific: identify the interest conveyed,
preserve the statute's continuing relationships and order-specific qualifications, and ask counsel to
state the result for this property.

::: {.recall}
1. What did the deed convey? **The land, in fee simple**, vested free and discharged from all
   encumbrances, read with the rest of the statute, any court order, the deed and the property
   record.
2. Which easement or right-of-way continues under the deed-effect rule? **The registered
   right-of-way serving the house behind may continue**: when the land being sold is burdened by an
   easement or right-of-way, the sale does not terminate or affect that continuing relationship as
   the Act specifies.
3. Was the sale shaped by a court order? **Confirm it.** If so, the sale takes its legal effect from
   the statute and the actual order, including any exceptions, exclusions or partial interests set
   out there.
4. Which remaining conclusion requires the lawyer's review of the exact PID and documents? **The
   result for this property**: the interest conveyed, what continues, and what the order qualifies.
:::

## The house does not empty on paper {#sec-08-possession}

One question still sits outside the title file.

::: {.case name="Foundry Street"}
Composite case — not a real property.

The fictional Foundry Street is not empty. Lights are on in two windows, a vehicle moves between
parking spaces, and mail continues to arrive. The deed has changed. The human scene has not
disappeared.
:::

### Who is there

Nothing in the exterior scene identifies the occupants. They might include a former owner, a tenant,
a family member, a guest, or someone whose situation does not fit a quick label. A parked vehicle
does not establish a tenancy, and a curtain in a window does not establish abandonment. Even a
conversation at the door would not give an untrained purchaser the complete legal facts.

Current CBRM guidance supplies the operational example: occupied properties can be sold, but that
municipality does not remove residents or provide access. Other events require their own current
municipal materials. In every case, the auction itself is not a substitute for the lawful,
fact-specific possession process. The deed is not a key handed across the registration counter.

### Vacant possession

This introduces **vacant possession**: the state in which the buyer can lawfully take possession of
the property without another person remaining entitled to occupy it. Whether and how that state can
be obtained depends on the actual facts and applicable law. It cannot be inferred from "immediate
deed," a paid balance, or the absence of a tenant's name in the sale advertisement.

::: {.rail label="A way to picture it"}
Title and possession resemble a name on a coat-check ticket and the coat in hand. The ticket
resembles evidence of entitlement. Holding the coat resembles actual control. One does not become
the other merely because the entitlement is strong.

The analogy stops there. Land, homes, tenancies, court remedies, and human safety are vastly more
complex than a coat check. The ticket does not authorize self-help, and neither does this
comparison.
:::

### Restraint before classification

::: careful
Foundry Street therefore calls for restraint before classification:

- No locks are changed.
- No utilities are interrupted.
- No belongings are moved.
- No demand for rent or departure is improvised at the door.

Those choices could affect people's rights and safety, destroy evidence about the occupancy
arrangement, or make a difficult legal problem worse.
:::

The research file can still become more precise. Counsel may need to determine:

- who is present;
- what documents or agreements exist;
- whether residential-tenancy law applies;
- what notices or proceedings are legally required;
- how access can be obtained.

The facts may lead to different processes for an owner who remains, a tenant, an apparently empty
unit containing goods, or an active dispute. This chapter deliberately does not select or teach one
of those processes.

### The same boundary in the redemption period

The same boundary reaches back into the redemption period. [Chapter 3](#sec-03-certificate)
explained that a certificate holder has limited statutory powers and duties, including protecting
the property, avoiding injury, and collecting rent in the circumstances the statute describes. Those
provisions assign responsibility. They do not turn uncertainty about an occupant into permission for
locks, confrontation, or an invented eviction route. The current document and legal stage still
control the holder's job.

### Goods left inside

Goods inside create another branch. A room that appears unused may still contain someone's
furniture, records, medicine, tools, or personal effects. Nova Scotia's residential-tenancy system
has specific resources and forms for tenant property left behind. Their existence demonstrates that
"looks abandoned" is not a disposal standard. The applicable facts and lawful process need to be
established before anyone treats goods as abandoned.

### Possession as a decision input

For a bidder still before the auction, possession becomes an input to the decision rather than an
afterthought:

- The likely cost and time of property-specific legal work may be unknown.
- Interior condition may remain unverified.
- Insurance, repairs, security, and intended use may depend on lawful access that the auction
  materials have not supplied.

A bidder who cannot tolerate those unresolved dependencies has learned something decisive without
approaching the door.

### When the asset is a manufactured home {#sec-08-manufactured}

Now change one fact. Instead of an occupied apartment building, the listing involves a manufactured
home. The familiar PID appears in the notice, and the map shows a land parcel beneath the symbol.
That is not enough to establish what asset is being sold.

Manufactured home: a separate question tied to different fictional records."}

Nova Scotia has specific tax-sale regulations and prescribed forms for mobile homes. The legal file
must first identify whether the sale concerns land, a mobile home, or some combination described by
the controlling documents.

An unaffixed home can also raise personal-property questions. Nova Scotia's **Personal Property
Registry** is the system used to search for security interests in personal property, including
mobile and manufactured homes. It is not the land registry. A clean-looking PID search does not
answer a search that belongs in the personal-property system.

Location introduces a third record family. A manufactured home may sit on land owned by someone else
in a **land-lease community**. Nova Scotia's Residential Tenancies framework applies to those
communities and includes rules about the space, the tenancy relationship, and sale of a home that
remains there.

Ownership of the home, ownership of the ground, the right to keep the home on the space, and any
registered personal-property security are distinct questions.

The bounded file reads:

::: {.filenote case="Harbour Park"}

land records, mobile-home records, and counsel whether the sale concerns the home, the land, or

land-lease relationship applies. No PID-only conclusion is supported.
:::

That note may feel slower than selecting the parcel and reading the aerial. It is faster than
discovering after payment that the mapped ground and visible home belong to different legal files.

### Who answers which question

Foundry Street and Harbour Park now share one recovery exercise.

::: {.recall}
1. What do you own? **The interest the deed conveys**, which counsel states for this property.
2. What burdens or benefits it? **The easements, rights-of-way and order-specific qualifications**
   that continue under the governing sources.
3. Who is there, and under what status? **A question for property and tenancy counsel**; the
   exterior scene does not identify the occupants.
4. If a mobile home is involved, what exactly is the asset, and which registry or tenancy system
   governs each part? **The home, the ground, the space and any security interest are distinct
   questions**, for the mobile-home records, the land records, the Residential Tenancies framework
   and the Personal Property Registry.
:::

The questions do not all need favourable answers. They need supportable answers or explicit stop
conditions before a bid.

| Who | What they can answer |
|---|---|
| Title counsel | Interpret the deed and land interests |
| Property and tenancy counsel | Identify the lawful possession route |
| The municipality | Clarify its current sale documents and event terms, without becoming the purchaser's lawyer or access provider |
| The Personal Property Registry and land records | Different searches |
| A map | Route the researcher to the right parcel and source; it cannot merge those legal systems |

The dramatic action in an occupied tax sale is often the action not taken:

- No lock turns because an auction concluded.
- No occupant becomes a trespasser by assumption.
- No box of belongings becomes garbage by appearance.
- No home and land become one asset because they occupy the same pixels.
:::

![**Observation stops before self-help.** Occupancy questions move from observation to legal advice, not to self-help: lawful exterior observation (remain off the parcel and avoid confrontation), then public records (preserve only bounded, source-backed facts), then lawyer or tenancy advice (establish the lawful route before contact or entry). statements apart protects the accuracy of the file and the people whose lives do not vanish when
ownership paperwork changes.
