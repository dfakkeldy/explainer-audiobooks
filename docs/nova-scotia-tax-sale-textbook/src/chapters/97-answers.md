# Answers to Check Your Understanding

### Chapter 1 · How a Property Reaches a Tax Sale {#ans-01}

#### 1.1 {#ans-01-1}

Preliminary notice (at least 14 days to pay); title search, after that period, with a possible
survey; notice of intent to the owner and interest holders (60 days to pay before the sale); public
advertisement (at least 30 consecutive days of qualifying public notice, by newspaper publication
that meets the statute or by posting on the municipal website); sale, by public auction or, with
council's consent, by tender. These are the steps outside Halifax under the Municipal Government
Act. See [The first clock](#sec-01-first-clock) and [Figure 1.2](#fig-01-pre-sale-timeline).

#### 1.2 {#ans-01-2}

Property must be put up for sale once taxes are unpaid for the three preceding fiscal years. The
requirement is subject to the Act's exceptions and to a possible council deferral. Inverness's
two-year description is not a competing deadline: the Act sets the provincial frame, and a
municipality may operate within that window using a shorter trigger. Inverness and Chester publicly
describe accounts as eligible after two years. See [When proceedings can
begin](#sec-01-first-clock).

#### 1.3 {#ans-01-3}

The line's job is to identify the property in a collection process and to tell the public where to
seek the fuller record; it is not trying to persuade anyone that Harbour Road (a fictional
composite) is desirable. A marker or parcel outline derived from an official notice helps you find
the event and the apparent parcel. It does not turn the municipality into the property's seller in
the ordinary real estate sense, and it does not turn the notice into a condition report. See
[Notice, not marketing](#sec-01-notice).

#### 1.4 {#ans-01-4}

Halifax's current notice, under its own Charter framework, controls. Halifax has a parallel
tax-sale framework in the Halifax Regional Municipality Charter rather than the tax-sale division of
the Municipal Government Act, so the correct authority is different even though the broad sequence is
recognizably similar. The packet can teach you what careful municipal information looks like, and
the map can route you to the right place; neither can silently replace the authority that owns the
event. See [Halifax](#sec-01-halifax) and [Figure 1.5](#fig-01-whose-rules).

#### 1.5 {#ans-01-5}

It shows that official records can disagree without either record becoming worthless (the same
packet also has lien 6 in the summary with no matching detailed sheet, and two recovery amounts
that differ between the summary and the detailed pages). It does not prove that a parcel is
defective, and it does not tell you which number controls on sale day. The researcher preserves
the conflict, names the two sources and asks the authority that owns the process (the
municipality), and does not choose the friendlier number because it makes the arithmetic work. See
[When official records disagree](#sec-01-packet).

#### 1.6 {#ans-01-6}

The parcel shape is not a boundary survey. The aerial photograph is not a current inspection. The
line saying that no dwelling is listed does not prove that the ground is empty, safe, buildable,
serviced, or available for immediate possession. Each item can still be valuable; none quietly
answers the other questions a bidder carries into the room. See [What each part can and cannot
establish](#sec-01-packet).

### Chapter 2 · Reading the Notice {#ans-02}

#### 2.1 {#ans-02-1}

The PID identifies the mapped parcel; the AAN identifies the assessment account. When Harbour
Road's summary row (a fictional composite) carries one AAN and one PID, the pairing is evidence that
the municipality associated a particular assessment account and parcel with the sale entry it
published. It is not proof that every building, driveway, fence, utility line or apparent yard
inside the reader's mental picture belongs to that parcel; each of those questions needs its own
source. See [Two catalogue keys](#sec-02-aan-pid) and [Figure 2.2](#fig-02-two-catalogue-keys).

#### 2.2 {#ans-02-2}

It supports "The municipality published $4,000 for this dated event under its stated terms." It
does not support "Harbour Road is worth more than $4,000," and it also does not support the opposite
conclusion. The recovery amount is built around the taxes, interest and sale expenses the
municipality seeks to recover; it was not produced by inspecting the property. Without title,
access, planning, condition, occupancy, tax, repair and uncertainty work, a reader cannot derive
value merely by objecting to the small municipal number. See [The recovery
amount](#sec-02-recovery).

#### 2.3 {#ans-02-3}

None of those secondary copies can settle it alone. The municipality's current notice, or direct
confirmation from the authority running the sale, says whether the lien remains in the current
event. Open the map's direct municipal source, check the current municipal notice or update, ask
the treasurer when the published sources still conflict, and record the retrieval time. The
earlier packet remains valuable evidence of what was published earlier; it does not become false
merely because the live process changed. See [A list that can shrink](#sec-02-list-shrink).

#### 2.4 {#ans-02-4}

It identifies the branch without the normal six-month statutory redemption right because of the
age of the arrears: the statutes remove that right when the taxes were already more than six years
in arrears at the time of sale. It does not establish possession, insurability, condition, clean
title or permission to use the land. A map's redemption filter cannot reclassify a parcel or
interpret the statute for a live decision; the source row, the current notice and qualified legal
advice remain the route when the classification matters. See [The redemption
marker](#sec-02-redemption-marker).

#### 2.5 {#ans-02-5}

One date records when the packet was retrieved; the other records the last event-status check.
Without dates, a file that mixes the original packet, a later municipal update, a map snapshot and
auction-day notes becomes misleading even when every source is authentic, because the reader no
longer knows which rows existed at which point in the process. The card also needs a source beside
every amount and marker: "Recovery amount" without a page and date is a number detached from its
authority, and "Redeemable" without the current notice is a label detached from its branch. See
[Dating the file](#sec-02-dating) and [Figure 2.5](#fig-02-source-state-labels).

### Chapter 3 · Certificate, Redemption and Deed {#ans-03}

#### 3.1 {#ans-03-1}

Leading bid, completed payment, registered certificate, tax deed. A leading bid is an auction event:
a raised card expresses a bid. Payment completes the purchaser's financial obligation under the sale
terms and leads to the purchaser's statutory position. For a redeemable property, the registered
certificate records the statutory position created by that completed sale and governs the
redeemable middle period. Only the last is the tax deed; none of the earlier stages is. Expiry
without redemption, or the older-arrears exception, permits the deed route. See [The document
between a bid and a deed](#sec-03-certificate) and [Two endings, two documents](#sec-03-endings).

#### 3.2 {#ans-03-2}

For most tax-sale properties, a qualifying owner or interest holder has six months after the sale to
redeem. Quarry Lane (a fictional composite) is in the older-arrears category: the normal statutory
redemption right does not apply when the taxes were already more than six years in arrears at sale,
so it follows the deed branch without the normal six-month redemption right. See [The middle
state](#sec-03-certificate) and [Figure 3.2](#fig-03-nonredeemable-route).

#### 3.3 {#ans-03-3}

The document is the registered certificate. The proposed action is protective rather than
developmental: the protective question is what work is necessary to prevent further damage, who
should authorize it, which insurer must be notified, and what records will later prove the nature
and cost of the work. The redemption right and any occupancy or tenancy obligations remain. The file
needs insurer communication, professional advice where required, photographs obtained lawfully,
invoices, proof of payment, and the municipality's approval where the statutory accounting calls for
it. The same facts do not support gutting the interior, removing healthy trees for a view, changing
the use, or treating the premises as vacant without evidence.
See [Four stage questions](#sec-03-certificate).

#### 3.4 {#ans-03-4}

An invoice proves that a contractor was paid. It does not by itself prove that the work was
necessary, that the amount belongs in the statutory redemption calculation, or that the required
approval was obtained. The insurance file, photographs, professional assessment, municipal
communication, invoice, and proof of payment perform different jobs. Every repair needs a reason
and the required approval, and the statute controls the accounting categories; the holder's labels
do not. See [Redemption and the evidence ledger](#sec-03-endings).

#### 3.5 {#ans-03-5}

Under the municipal statutes, the tax deed vests fee-simple title free of encumbrances, subject to
the statutes' treatment of continuing easements and rights-of-way. Those words require legal
interpretation against the actual parcel and records and should not be shortened into "clean
title". The deed does not certify a boundary, create road access, approve development, remove an
occupant, supply insurance, or guarantee that the title will never be contested. Immediate deed is a
description of timing within the redemption system: the normal six-month redemption period is absent
for the older-arrears category. "Immediate" does not promise that every practical and legal question
can be resolved on sale day. See [The deed branch](#sec-03-endings).

### Chapter 4 · Building the Parcel File {#ans-04}

#### 4.1 {#ans-04-1}

For each detailed entry, the Inverness packet gathers account and parcel identifiers, assessment
figures, mapped views and a legal description. A researcher who begins by copying those fields into
a prettier page has changed the presentation, not the state of knowledge. The inventory asks which
facts came from the municipal packet, which source the municipality named, and which details
describe the auction event, which describe an assessment account, and which purport to describe the
parcel. It credits the municipality and
prevents later research from being mistaken for original discovery. See [The
inventory](#sec-04-baseline) and [Figure 4.1](#fig-04-beyond-the-packet).

#### 4.2 {#ans-04-2}

A clean-looking research report could hide the conflict by choosing one number. Inverness's August
packet has a summary lien with no matching detailed sheet, and two recovery amounts differ between
the summary and detail portions. A parcel biography preserves both records, identifies their dates
and locations, and routes the current-event question back to the municipality. See [The source
ledger](#sec-04-baseline).

#### 4.3 {#ans-04-3}

The search or parcel-register view is a dated snapshot. The source instrument, release, agreement or
order supplies the underlying terms. The legal effect of that material, including what the tax-deed
provisions do or do not change, requires the governing law and property-specific interpretation. The
appearance of a mortgage, judgment, easement or other interest proves neither its present amount nor
its release, priority or treatment after sale. See [Property Online and the parcel
register](#sec-04-baseline).

#### 4.4 {#ans-04-4}

The municipality performs title work as part of the statutory notice process, and that work helps
identify people entitled to notice. The municipality is moving a tax-enforcement process through its
required stages; the bidder is deciding whether the legal position, uncertainty, project and price
belong together. The municipal title work does not become a purchaser's title opinion merely because
both inquiries involve the same parcel: the questions overlap, but the professional duty and
requested conclusion do not. A prospective bidder may need a lawyer to examine the records for the
bidder's own decision. See [Title search](#sec-04-baseline).

#### 4.5 {#ans-04-5}

The no-go entry governs, even though the aerial image is attractive. Evidence is not a vote in which
three comfortable facts outnumber one unresolved essential fact; the importance of the unknown
depends on the project it can defeat. The no-go label is not a prediction that the land is bad. It
describes the fit between the available evidence, the deadline, the researcher's tolerance and the
proposed use, and the correct label is "do not proceed under these conditions." Birch Point Road
is a composite case, not a real property. See [One essential no-go
governs](#sec-04-four-destinations) and [Figure 4.5](#fig-04-four-destinations).

### Chapter 5 · Using the Map as a Research Tool {#ans-05}

#### 5.1 {#ans-05-1}

The counts are a dated catalogue snapshot from the production build checked on July 22, 2026. They
are not a claim that the event, or every entry, remains live when you read them. The app is a
research index. The municipality's current notice or direct confirmation controls whether the sale
is live, a parcel remains listed, the event has been postponed, or a term has changed. A copied PDF,
screenshot, browser tab or map catalogue can become stale, so the direct source link is part of the
operating sequence. If the direct notice and the app disagree, the disagreement is recorded; the app
does not break the tie in its own favour. See [Current notice or historical
result](#sec-05-record-family) and [Figure 5.2](#fig-05-current-vs-historical).

#### 5.2 {#ans-05-2}

Containment is a precise interface operation: this returned civic point falls inside this returned
parcel polygon. It does not prove who owns or occupies the parcel,
whether the point is the mailing address for every interest, whether a building sits at the point,
whether the public can enter, or whether there is legal access from the road. Containment finds the
parcel to investigate; it does not finish the investigation. See [Starting from a civic
address](#sec-05-record-family).

#### 5.3 {#ans-05-3}

The building count reports Nova Scotia Topographic Database point or polygon features intersecting
the mapped parcel. It does not establish how many structures currently exist, whether any are
occupied, their condition or use, or whether they have permits; a returned zero does not prove an
empty parcel either. The 2026 assessed and taxable values of $35,000 are a dated PVSC assessment.
The amount is not today's sale price, a current appraisal, a repair estimate or a bid ceiling. The
demonstration parcel is not presented as a current tax-sale listing. See [Parcel
identity](#sec-05-record-family) and [Figure 5.5](#fig-05-buildings-assessment).

#### 5.4 {#ans-05-4}

No. "No mapped intersection" does not mean no access. A right-of-way may exist outside the road
dataset; a visible route may be private, unmapped, misaligned or legally irrelevant; and the parcel
geometry itself is not a survey. Visible transportation nearby does not fill the gap either:
nearness answers a spatial orientation question, while legal access answers whether a recognized
right permits the required passage. The result routes the file toward title, plan, survey and
municipal road questions; the note names land records and a lawyer for access. See [Reading the road result](#sec-05-one-question).

#### 5.5 {#ans-05-5}

If the note loses its limitation, the result becomes too strong: nothing says that both source
geometry and parcel geometry are screening data. If it loses the unknown and handoff, it creates
caution without progress: nothing names what remains open (legal access, ground conditions, water
constraints, current service completeness) or who can answer it (land records and a lawyer for
access; survey and current municipal or environmental sources for the physical and regulatory
questions). Source, date, observation, limitation, unknown and handoff travel together. See [A note
in six parts](#sec-05-one-question) and [Figure 5.11](#fig-05-six-part-note).

#### 5.6 {#ans-05-6}

The Halifax figures answer a bounded historical question: what did the checked official records
report for that PID at that event? They do not answer what the parcel is worth now. The result is
not a comparable sale model, does not predict competition at another auction, does not establish a
discount and does not supply a bidding ratio; tax-sale outcomes occur inside their own legal,
physical, market, information and participation conditions. The CBRM record for PID `15234636`, from
the July 21, 2026 event, shows the minimum bid and notice fields, but its winning-bid field says
"Awaiting official results." It claims no sale outcome, no purchaser and no inference about present
ownership or parcel status: moving a dated notice into historical mode after its event date does not
manufacture a result the municipality has not published. See [Historical mode](#sec-05-historical).

### Chapter 6 · Access and Intended Use {#ans-06}

#### 6.1 {#ans-06-1}

Getting near the land is a physical observation. Having a right to reach it is a legal conclusion
that must be supported. Having a route capable of serving the project is a further physical and
regulatory question. A visible track answers only the first. See [The access note](#sec-06-route) and
[Figure 6.2](#fig-06-case-a-access).

#### 6.2 {#ans-06-2}

The plan does not say by itself whether the line was surveyed as a boundary, drawn as an existing
road, proposed as a route, or tied to a registered interest. The researcher must also ask whether
the current parcel benefits, whether the description is clear enough to locate, and whether later work has changed the
physical route. The plan, related instruments, current survey evidence, and legal interpretation
must be reconciled rather than ranked by which picture looks most convincing. See [Changing the
facts](#sec-06-route).

#### 6.3 {#ans-06-3}

Road frontage is the parcel's relationship to a road as defined and required by the applicable
rules. A right to pass over a neighbour's land may solve one legal access problem while leaving a
separate frontage or development-standard problem. The registered passage may permit vehicles to
reach the land, while the development rules may still require the lot itself, or a proposed
alteration to it, to satisfy a particular road relationship. The planning authority must answer the
rule question for the proposed project. See [Road
frontage](#sec-06-use).

#### 6.4 {#ans-06-4}

The useful question names the exact PID, the proposed use, the available access evidence, and the
specific confirmation needed before a bid. A planner cannot test a vague hope. The call discloses the
access gap rather than inviting an answer built on an unstated assumption, and its wording avoids
asking the planner to guarantee a project. See [The planning call](#sec-06-use).

#### 6.5 {#ans-06-5}

A permitted use removes one possible prohibition. It does not establish that the parcel is a
buildable lot, that a compliant building envelope exists, that the route can serve the dwelling, or
that water and sewage approvals will be available. Encouraging facts do not merge into a permit. If
the file still holds an essential dependency the bidder cannot resolve before the auction (an
ambiguous access instrument, say, or a planning authority that cannot confirm the lot and route can
support the use without later technical work), either is enough to stop. That stop is a
conclusion about this bidder's evidence, deadline, and intended use, not a prediction that the
parcel is worthless. See [A rational no](#sec-06-use) and
[Figure 6.5](#fig-06-independent-gates).

### Chapter 7 · Physical and Environmental Records {#ans-07}

#### 7.1 {#ans-07-1}

The observation should remain literal: a feature is visible, or a historical record describes a
use. For the fictional Breakwater Lane, the file says that a small building and a possible former
tank area are visible from public imagery, and records the date and source of the image. Those
observations do not establish a spill, contaminant, tank location, or present condition.
"Contamination" is not an exterior observation. See [Keep the observation
literal](#sec-07-three-records).

#### 7.2 {#ans-07-2}

An empty search establishes that the researcher did not find a responsive record within the chosen
source, terms, date, and search method. It does not establish that no release occurred, that no
contaminant exists, that every historic activity was reported, or that the parcel is clean. A
registry is a collection of records created under its own rules and history, not a soil sample
spread across the province. The Breakwater Lane note's next question is to expand the address,
parcel-history, neighbouring-site, and prior-use search, then ask an environmental professional what
lawful pre-purchase assessment is warranted. See [Contaminated sites and the Environmental
Registry](#sec-07-three-records).

#### 7.3 {#ans-07-3}

No. The map point is not the well. Its position may be based on the location information available
when the record was collected, and the Province warns that the database may be incomplete or
inaccurate. Containment compares two mapped objects; it does not cure uncertainty in the source
coordinates or prove that the physical feature remains where the log suggests. A log also records a
drilling event, not a guarantee of current equipment, current yield, potable water, legal
compliance, or the condition of whatever exists on site today. The current well location, parcel
association, ownership, physical condition, yield, water quality, and serviceability remain
unverified. See [Wells and well logs](#sec-07-three-records).

#### 7.4 {#ans-07-4}

Nova Scotia's environmental record service warns that a record is unlikely for a property more than
seven years old, and its process information describes on-site sewage files as records with a
limited retention period. The negative result is consistent with many realities: an older system
installed under records no longer retained; a system changed, abandoned, shared, or never documented
in the searched source; a changed address or parcel relationship; a file elsewhere. So it does not
establish that no system exists, that an existing system was approved, or that any system is
suitable or functional now. Current municipal and provincial requirements, available property
history, and qualified on-site assessment remain separate next steps. See [On-site sewage
records](#sec-07-three-records).

#### 7.5 {#ans-07-5}

For the coastal result: the checked Coastal Hazard Map shows scenario coverage overlapping part of
the current graphical parcel under the selected 2100 setting; this is a long-range screening
observation, not a surveyed boundary, site elevation, event prediction, engineering conclusion,
insurance determination, or permit decision. It should not say "The property will flood."

For the mine result: the checked source returned no displayed opening under the recorded query and
map state. Because the inventory is incomplete and excludes some expressions of hazard, the result
cannot support "no mining hazard"; it may still reduce one specific concern, that no known inventory
point was returned by that search. See [The Coastal Hazard Map](#sec-07-shore-and-past) and
[Abandoned mine openings](#sec-07-shore-and-past).

#### 7.6 {#ans-07-6}

It is an error state: the researcher does not have a completed result. A failed service, loading
timeout, hidden layer, or incorrect selection cannot be narrated as "nothing found." The note should
preserve the failure, and the researcher should retry or use the authoritative source directly. Draw
no negative conclusion. See [Positive, negative and error](#sec-07-shore-and-past).

### Chapter 8 · Title and Possession {#ans-08}

#### 8.1 {#ans-08-1}

Under Nova Scotia's Municipal Government Act, the tax deed is evidence that the statutory sale
provisions were followed, and the Act says the deed vests the land in the grantee in fee simple,
free and discharged from all encumbrances. Fee simple is the broad form of private ownership estate
ordinarily meant when people speak of owning land. The phrase describes the legal interest conveyed.
It does not describe the building's condition, identify every person inside, certify a development
plan, or turn a graphical parcel boundary into a survey. See [Fee simple](#sec-08-deed).

#### 8.2 {#ans-08-2}

The statute itself prevents the summary that the deed erased everything. When the land being sold
is burdened by an easement or right-of-way, the sale does not terminate or affect that continuing
relationship as the Act specifies, so Foundry Street's registered right-of-way may continue. The
deed may discharge interests the statute discharges, but which ones is for counsel to confirm. The
title file needs the exact deed, the current parcel register, the instruments behind relevant
entries, the legal description, and a lawyer's interpretation of how the governing provisions apply
to this parcel; for a continuing right-of-way, also its route, purpose, width, parties, maintenance
language, and any other terms the source instruments actually contain, never reconstructed from a
driveway line in imagery. See [One right-of-way, two pieces of land](#sec-08-deed) and
[Figure 8.2](#fig-08-deed-effect).

#### 8.3 {#ans-08-3}

One describes the legal effect the statute gives a qualifying deed. The other refuses to promise
that a municipality's auction page has completed a property-specific title opinion, found every
factual problem, resolved possession, or made the interest readily insurable or marketable for a
particular transaction. Questions such as which statute governs, whether there was a court order and
what it said, and which easements or rights-of-way benefit or burden the land belong with a Nova
Scotia property lawyer reviewing the actual file. See [Why "clear title" is an unsafe
label](#sec-08-deed).

#### 8.4 {#ans-08-4}

Vacant possession is the state in which the buyer can lawfully take possession of the property
without another person remaining entitled to occupy it. Whether and how it can be obtained depends
on the actual facts and applicable law; it cannot be inferred from "immediate deed," a paid balance,
or the absence of a tenant's name in the sale advertisement. Nothing in the exterior scene
identifies the occupants. No locks are changed, no utilities are interrupted, no belongings are
moved, and no demand for rent or departure is improvised at the door: those choices could affect
people's rights and safety, destroy evidence about the occupancy arrangement, or make a difficult
legal problem worse. The questions for counsel are who is present, what documents or agreements
exist, whether residential-tenancy law applies, what notices or proceedings are legally required,
and how access can be obtained. See [The house does not empty on paper](#sec-08-possession).

#### 8.5 {#ans-08-5}

The file must first identify whether the sale concerns land, a mobile home, or some combination
described by the controlling documents; Nova Scotia has specific tax-sale regulations and prescribed
forms for mobile homes. Ownership of the home, ownership of the ground, the right to keep the home
on the space, and any registered personal-property security are distinct questions. Security
interests in the home are searched in the Personal Property Registry, which is not the land
registry; if the home sits in a land-lease community, Nova Scotia's Residential Tenancies framework
applies. The bounded file confirms the asset from the current municipal notice, prescribed sale
documents, land records, mobile-home records, and counsel. No PID-only conclusion is supported. See
[When the asset is a manufactured home](#sec-08-manufactured) and
[Figure 8.5](#fig-08-manufactured-home-records).

### Chapter 9 · Results, Costs and the Maximum Bid {#ans-09}

#### 9.1 {#ans-09-1}

The municipality advertised 50 properties; later council minutes report that 35 sold and 15 were
removed before the sale after collection or legal advice; the public result sheet contains 31 bid
rows. Fifty describes what was advertised, 35 the sales reported to council, and 31 the rows
available for calculation in the public result sheet. The four-sale difference between the reported
total and the published rows remains unresolved. A researcher who silently calls the 31-row sheet
"all sales" has changed the source rather than clarified it. See
[Three counts for one event](#sec-09-result-sheet) and
[Figure 9.1](#fig-09-fifty-thirtyfive-thirtyone).

#### 9.2 {#ans-09-2}

It establishes that competition often moved well beyond the municipal recovery number in that dated
sample. It does not establish that the higher bids were correct, that the lower bids were bargains,
or that a future Inverness auction will behave the same way. See
[What the 31 rows show](#sec-09-result-sheet) and
[Figure 9.2](#fig-09-inverness-ratio-distribution).

#### 9.3 {#ans-09-3}

Each comparison carries the municipality, event date, sale format, published-row count,
denominator, and known completeness limit. A minimum bid in one event and listed charges in another
may have similar economic roles without being perfectly interchangeable fields. Descriptive samples
can challenge folklore while remaining unsuitable for prediction; Richmond County's three June 2026
rows, for example, are far too few to support a municipal price rule. See
[Comparing municipalities](#sec-09-result-sheet) and
[Figure 9.3](#fig-09-municipal-result-comparison).

#### 9.4 {#ans-09-4}

An unknown foundation repair might support a reserve when exterior and professional evidence
provide a meaningful range. Unverified legal access for a project that requires road access may
remain a no-go. Unknown occupancy costs may be unpriceable when the lawful process and timing cannot
be established. The reserve catches bounded variation; it does not bridge a missing legal right or
manufacture permission, and an unpriceable essential item needs a stop condition, not a larger
miscellaneous line. See [The uncertainty reserve](#sec-09-build-backward).

#### 9.5 {#ans-09-5}

The four branches are the municipal tax-sale-deed exemption from municipal deed transfer tax, the
provincial non-resident deed transfer tax, the HST treatment of the particular transaction, and the
buyer's eligibility under current federal, provincial, and municipal law. CBRM's current event
instructions say HST applies to vacant land and commercially assessed property. That is an important
rule for a CBRM bidder to clarify and fund, but Canada Revenue Agency guidance shows that
real-property treatment can depend on the seller, prior use, property, transaction, and purchaser's
registration status. The cost file should record the event's terms and obtain case-specific tax
advice. See [Tax and eligibility branches](#sec-09-build-backward) and
[Figure 9.5](#fig-09-tax-eligibility-branches).

#### 9.6 {#ans-09-6}

The bidder begins with the supported value boundary: the highest all-in amount the bidder can defend
for the stated use under explicit title, condition and market assumptions, established from
property-specific valuation evidence. Known non-bid costs and the uncertainty reserve consume part of
that boundary; what remains can become the maximum bid if every essential legal, eligibility,
payment, and no-go condition is satisfied, and it must be fundable within the statutory payment
structure. If nothing remains, the research has produced a no-bid result. The boundary is not the
assessment, recovery amount, a historical bid or the price another participant appears willing to
pay. When another card rises past the written maximum, nothing about the property has changed; the
only new fact is that another person will pay more, so the bidder lowers the card. That executes the
decision the research supported. See [The maximum bid](#sec-09-build-backward) and
[Figure 9.6](#fig-09-build-backward).

### Chapter 10 · Auction Day, Tenders and Payment {#ans-10}

#### 10.1 {#ans-10-1}

An open-outcry auction is a live public process in which participants respond to the bids called
in the room until no higher acceptable bid is offered. A public tender is a defined process in which
bidders submit offers by the stated deadline and the municipality opens and evaluates them under the
event's terms; the competing offers are not adjusted through live calls. In the room, the temptation is
to chase a visible rival. At a desk, the temptation is to raise a sealed offer to avoid losing by an imagined
small difference. Neither temptation adds title, access, condition, possession, tax or use evidence,
so the fictional Cedar Street maximum enters either format unchanged. Procedure changes how an offer
is delivered; it does not enlarge what the file supports. See [Two formats, one
decision](#sec-10-two-formats).

#### 10.2 {#ans-10-2}

The sheet still separates three questions: Is the bidder legally eligible to acquire this kind of
property? Does the bidder or the agent have documented authority to make this particular bid? Has the
bidder complied with the event's registration or tender-submission instructions? A yes in one box
does not fill the other two. The Municipal Government Act's conflict rule prohibits a council member
or employee of the selling municipality, specified village commission members or employees, their
spouses, and a company in which one of those people owns or beneficially owns a majority of the
outstanding shares from buying at that municipality's tax sale, directly or through an agent. That
rule is narrower than a complete eligibility review: it does not answer the federal, tax,
corporate-authority or representation questions from Chapter 9. See [Who may not
buy](#sec-10-two-formats).

#### 10.3 {#ans-10-3}

Maya's payment sheet identifies the current event, the amount required immediately, the accepted
method in hand, the account or instrument that can complete the balance, the three-business-day
deadline and the person authorized to act. It serves two readiness tests: the immediate amount must
be available in an accepted form when the sale requires it, and the rest must be available through a
verified path within three business days. A bank draft for one amount is not automatically
adjustable in the room, and a debit card is useful only if the municipality accepts it and the
financial institution permits the transaction. Council's power to authorize another legal-tender
delivery method does not require a particular event to accept every possible method; the current
sale terms decide what the municipality will take and how it must be delivered. If one required field is
unverified, the parcel is not ready even when the maximum appears affordable. See [The finish line
beyond the hammer](#sec-10-finish-line).

#### 10.4 {#ans-10-4}

Under the Municipal Government Act, the treasurer may bid the taxes, interest and expenses and
purchase the land for the municipality. "May" matters: no sufficient private bid does not force the
municipality to acquire every parcel. If the municipality does not purchase, it may advertise the
property again and later sell it at auction for the best obtainable price or by highest tender,
subject to any acceptable minimum directed by council.
"Unsold" does not mean available at the recovery amount, over the counter or on conditions selected
by the observer. CBRM's result legend, as checked in July 2026, states that an unsold property may be
purchased only at a future tax sale; another municipality's next step must be checked in its own
current record. See [When the expected sale does not happen](#sec-10-breaks).

#### 10.5 {#ans-10-5}

When the balance does not arrive within three business days, the Act sends the land back to
advertisement and sale. Resale expenses are deducted from the deposit, and the remaining balance of
the deposit is refunded after the resale is held. The amount and timing of that result depend on the
actual municipal process; the book cannot calculate them for a bidder in advance. Meanwhile the
bidder's funds remain tied to a future event. A maximum that was affordable only if financing could
be arranged afterward was never operationally affordable. See [When the expected sale does not
happen](#sec-10-breaks).

### Chapter 11 · The Certificate-Holder Months {#ans-11}

#### 11.1 {#ans-11-1}

The Municipal Government Act gives a certificate holder the owner-like powers needed to protect the
land. The holder may collect rents due or becoming due and may use the land without diminishing its
value. The same section forbids cutting trees, injuring the premises, or knowingly allowing another
person to do so, and it requires the purchaser to insure buildings if they are insurable. At the
fictional Cedar Street, a leaking opening needs protection from the next rain: work aimed at
arresting an immediate loss is protective. Removing mature trees for a future view is outside the
lane, and a full redesign for Maya's proposed rehabilitation can wait: work aimed at changing the
property for a future use is development. That distinction does not authorize either action by
itself; entry, occupancy, tenancy, safety, permits, contractor authority and the exact condition
still matter. See [Responsibility before certainty](#sec-11-responsibility).

#### 11.2 {#ans-11-2}

Cedar Street appears vacant from public-road observations, and the word "appears" stays in the file.
Curtains, vehicles, utilities, stored goods or a quiet building cannot prove lawful vacancy. Maya does
not force entry, change locks, confront a person or direct somebody to remove belongings. She asks
counsel how the certificate power interacts with the actual possession facts and records the answer
before any physical step. An urgent threat is routed to the appropriate emergency or municipal
authority, and non-urgent condition work is scoped by qualified people using lawful access. See
[Responsibility before certainty](#sec-11-responsibility).

#### 11.3 {#ans-11-3}

The refusal is useful evidence. It does not prove that the building is uninsurable in the statutory
sense, because one company's underwriting decision is not a legal determination for every market,
and it does not permit Maya to declare the duty impossible and stop looking without advice. The
deemed insurable interest explains why Maya may insure; it does not command an insurance company to
accept Cedar Street, define the coverage, or make the premium affordable. Maya dates the refusal,
keeps it with the questions asked and routes it back into her cost and risk file. If a broker later
identifies a possible form of coverage subject to conditions, she obtains the actual terms and
professional advice before treating that response as coverage: a quote is not a bound policy. See
[Responsibility before certainty](#sec-11-responsibility).

#### 11.4 {#ans-11-4}

The work does not silently expand. The new condition returns through the same authority,
professional and insurance channels. The statute treats reimbursement for repairs narrowly: the
redemption amount may include amounts the purchaser paid for necessary repairs to buildings when
those repairs were made with the treasurer's written approval. The approved repair connects an observed risk to a professional scope, a written municipal
approval, a lawful performance route and an amount actually paid. Remove any one of those links and
the reimbursement claim becomes a different question; good intentions cannot reconstruct missing
authority after redemption begins. See [Responsibility before certainty](#sec-11-responsibility).

#### 11.5 {#ans-11-5}

Under the Municipal Government Act, the starting category is the sum the purchaser paid. Interest at
10 percent per year runs on the total paid by the purchaser from the sale date to the redemption
date. The formula can also include certain older unpaid taxes when the purchase payment did not
cover them, taxes levied after the sale and related interest, the fee to record the discharge,
qualifying fire-insurance premiums and necessary-repair amounts paid with the treasurer's written
approval. Any balance remaining in the tax-sale surplus account for the property is deducted, as is
rent or other income earned by the purchaser from the land. The treasurer determines the amount
required. From the time the full redemption amount is paid to the treasurer, the Act says the
purchaser ceases to have a right to the land. A statement that someone plans to redeem does not end
the purchaser's interest, and delivery of part of the amount does not end it either. See [Closing the
ledger when the property is redeemed](#sec-11-redemption).

### Chapter 12 · After the Deed {#ans-12}

#### 12.1 {#ans-12-1}

If the taxes were already unpaid for more than six years when the sale occurred, the purchaser may
request the deed after the sale. For a redeemable property that remains unredeemed, the request
comes after six months. In either route, the purchaser pays the fee set by council and the
municipality delivers the prescribed deed to the purchaser or as the purchaser directs. It is a
request-and-document step, not an automatic page turning on the sixth-month anniversary. The
fictional Foundry Street file confirms that the redemption period has actually expired without
redemption, obtains the municipality's current instructions, pays the applicable fee and checks the
delivered deed: the land description, grantee, signatures, municipal seal, related sale record and
any direction given by the purchaser. See [The document changes, the questions
move](#sec-12-document).

#### 12.2 {#ans-12-2}

The Municipal Government Act exempts a deed given through a tax sale from municipal deed transfer
tax, but the exemption does not answer every tax question. The separate provincial non-resident
deed transfer tax and the transaction-specific HST branch remain property- and purchaser-specific,
so a lawyer and tax adviser apply the current rules to the actual transfer. The tax exemption also
does not erase the required registration information: as of July 2026, the provincial deed-transfer
service says an Affidavit of Value is required when real property is transferred, even when no
municipal deed transfer tax is payable. Land Registry fees are due when a document is registered,
recorded or filed, and those amounts can change; the file keeps the current official fee, payment
receipt, submitted documents, registration particulars and lawyer's confirmation. See [The document
changes, the questions move](#sec-12-document).

#### 12.3 {#ans-12-3}

The six-month redemption period runs from the sale date, for a redeemable property; if the taxes
were unpaid for more than six years when the sale occurred, there is no six-month period. The window
to apply to the Supreme Court for surplus proceeds also runs from the sale date: after the redemption
period and before 20 years have passed from the sale. The Marketable Titles Act period runs from the
deed-registration date: a tax deed generally may be set aside only during the six years following its
registration, subject to the land-exclusion conditions, the current-owner fraud or breach-of-trust
exception and the preserved damages claim. It does not start on auction day, at the end of
redemption, at deed delivery or on first entry. Six years is not a title-review waiting strategy.
See [Figure 12.1](#fig-12-three-clocks).

#### 12.4 {#ans-12-4}

In CBRM's July 2026 description, the zoning letter confirms the zoning and applicable provisions
identified by municipal planning staff, and the clearance letter reports specified outstanding
building-code, by-law or fire-code violations in municipal records. Neither letter is a universal
property approval. A zoning confirmation does not promise that a proposed design will receive
permits. A clearance response does not replace an inspection and cannot reveal a defect that was
never reported or recorded. The owner keeps the response date and qualifications and sends any
difficult answer to the appropriate municipal staff or professional. See [The second due-diligence
wave](#sec-12-second-wave).

#### 12.5 {#ans-12-5}

A person claiming an interest in land sold for taxes may apply to the Supreme Court after the
redemption period and before 20 years have passed from the sale, and the court may order
proportional payment from the tax-sale surplus account. That is a route, not a prediction about
Elena's standing, evidence, share or result; her lawyer identifies the correct parties, proof and
court procedure. The surplus account is a proceeds record, not a statement about which interests
continue to burden the land, so Elena does not treat her old interest as a continuing lien on the
land, and the deed holder does not appropriate surplus money that the statute reserves for another
process. A possible proceeds claim does not grant present possession or control of the land. See
[The money and the challenge take different roads](#sec-12-money).

### Chapter 13 · Putting the Method Together {#ans-13}

#### 13.1 {#ans-13-1}

The verified fact is that the dated notice lists one account with two PIDs and that the returned
civic point is contained in one of the two graphical parcel polygons. The unresolved question is what
interest the municipality intends to sell and how the two PIDs, account, legal descriptions and any
building relate. The next authorities are the municipality's current notice or confirmation and the
governed land-record review interpreted by counsel. The consequence is a stop. No parcel may be
silently dropped from the file merely because the civic point and roof make one shape more
memorable: civic-point containment found a parcel to investigate; it did not rewrite a two-PID notice
into a one-parcel sale. Until the municipal record and land-record evidence reconcile the two PIDs,
the file cannot support a title request, use analysis or maximum bid. See [Three parcels, three
different endings](#sec-13-three-endings).

#### 13.2 {#ans-13-2}

The building image does not identify who is present, the person's legal status or whether vacant
possession can be obtained. The historical business reference does not diagnose contamination. The
negative registry search means only that this search found no matching record in that source; it
does not certify the soil, water, air, building materials or contents. The assessed-value record
records a dated mass-appraisal and taxation context, including the assessment classification shown
by the public source; it is not a current appraisal of the occupied building, an inspection of the
former use or a prediction of the auction result. Nothing in the fictional notice supplies resident
removal or entry. See [Three parcels, three different endings](#sec-13-three-endings).

#### 13.3 {#ans-13-3}

The researcher does not invent reserves large enough to make the unknowns disappear. With no lawful
inspection opportunity, unresolved occupancy status and no time for an environmental professional to
review the former-use evidence, the unknowns are not yet priceable. The file records why they are not
yet priceable and which facts would be required to reopen it. Occupancy belongs in a lawyer-reviewed
possession and tenancy file; former use and current condition belong in a property-specific
environmental and building review; an insurer can respond to the actual use, occupancy and condition
evidence; tax treatment needs its own route. The bidder owns the decision to stop because essential
information cannot be obtained in time. See [Three parcels,
three different endings](#sec-13-three-endings).

#### 13.4 {#ans-13-4}

The graphical boundary remains an orientation record rather than a survey. The road relationship
supports a focused access question rather than a legal right. The zoning response identifies current
zoning and applicable provisions; it is not a permit, service confirmation or promise that the
intended project will be approved. Empty environmental, well, sewage, coastal or mine searches retain
the coverage and currency limits of their sources. The routes: the lawyer can review the parcel
register, legal description and access evidence; the municipality can answer the exact frontage,
lot, intended-use and approval questions; service and site professionals can test what the public
record cannot; a surveyor can be engaged if the boundary or road relationship matters to the plan.
The file does not need every possible fact; it needs every decision-critical uncertainty either
resolved or assigned a defensible treatment. See [Three parcels, three different
endings](#sec-13-three-endings).

#### 13.5 {#ans-13-5}

Payment readiness remains a separate gate. A parcel can fit the evidence and budget while the bidder
lacks the authority, accepted funds or balance path required by the current event terms; that file is
not ready. The maximum must also be written: the supported value boundary and the all-in costs
produce it, and auction energy cannot raise it. Even when the evidence may support participation under
a fixed maximum and verified payment plan, the researcher can explain why the file reached that point
but still cannot declare the parcel a bargain, recommend a bid or decide that the bidder should
proceed. See [Three parcels, three different endings](#sec-13-three-endings).

#### 13.6 {#ans-13-6}

The file opens the certificate-holder records and stops speaking about a possible bid. The new stage
record states the controlling document, the power or duty it creates, its boundary, the next
authorized action and the event that will end that stage; the old evidence remains dated evidence of
what was known and does not continue to authorize action. Full redemption payment to the treasurer
ends the purchaser's rights. If the sale is not redeemed, it can proceed to the deed request and a new
ownership file when the applicable wait and municipal process permit it. The registered deed has real
statutory effect, but it still does not make the old map a survey, remove occupants, inspect the
building, create a right-of-way or approve development. See [The record that survives the
event](#sec-13-record).
