## Chapter 3 · Certificate, Redemption and Deed {#ans-03}

### 3.1 {#ans-03-1}

Leading bid, completed payment, registered certificate, tax deed. A leading bid is an auction event:
a raised card expresses a bid. Payment completes the purchaser's financial obligation under the sale
terms and leads to the purchaser's statutory position. For a redeemable property, the registered
certificate records the statutory position created by that completed sale and governs the
redeemable middle period. Only the last is the tax deed; none of the earlier stages is. Expiry
without redemption, or the older-arrears exception, permits the deed route. See [The document
between a bid and a deed](#sec-03-certificate) and [Two endings, two documents](#sec-03-endings).

### 3.2 {#ans-03-2}

For most tax-sale properties, a qualifying owner or interest holder has six months after the sale to
redeem. Quarry Lane (a fictional composite) is in the older-arrears category: the normal statutory
redemption right does not apply when the taxes were already more than six years in arrears at sale,
so it follows the deed branch without the normal six-month redemption right. See [The middle
state](#sec-03-certificate) and [Figure 3.2](#fig-03-nonredeemable-route).

### 3.3 {#ans-03-3}

The document is the registered certificate. The proposed action is protective rather than
developmental: the protective question is what work is necessary to prevent further damage, who
should authorize it, which insurer must be notified, and what records will later prove the nature
and cost of the work. The redemption right and any occupancy or tenancy obligations remain. The file
needs insurer communication, professional advice where required, photographs obtained lawfully,
invoices, proof of payment, and the municipality's approval where the statutory accounting calls for
it. The same facts do not support gutting the interior, removing healthy trees for a view, changing
the use, or treating the premises as vacant without evidence. The four questions are a stage check,
not a do-it-yourself legal test. See [Four stage questions](#sec-03-certificate).

### 3.4 {#ans-03-4}

An invoice proves that a contractor was paid. It does not by itself prove that the work was
necessary, that the amount belongs in the statutory redemption calculation, or that the required
approval was obtained. The insurance file, photographs, professional assessment, municipal
communication, invoice, and proof of payment perform different jobs. Every repair needs a reason
and the required approval, and the statute controls the accounting categories; the holder's labels
do not. See [Redemption and the evidence ledger](#sec-03-endings).

### 3.5 {#ans-03-5}

Under the municipal statutes, the tax deed vests fee-simple title free of encumbrances, subject to
the statutes' treatment of continuing easements and rights-of-way. Those words require legal
interpretation against the actual parcel and records and should not be shortened into "clean
title". The deed does not certify a boundary, create road access, approve development, remove an
occupant, supply insurance, or guarantee that the title will never be contested. Immediate deed is a
description of timing within the redemption system: the normal six-month redemption period is absent
for the older-arrears category. "Immediate" does not promise that every practical and legal question
can be resolved on sale day. See [The deed branch](#sec-03-endings).
