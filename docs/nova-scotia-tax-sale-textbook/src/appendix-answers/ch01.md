## Chapter 1 · How a Property Reaches a Tax Sale {#ans-01}

### 1.1 {#ans-01-1}

Preliminary notice (at least 14 days to pay); title search, after that period, with a possible
survey; notice of intent to the owner and interest holders (60 days to pay before the sale); public
advertisement (at least 30 consecutive days of qualifying public notice, by newspaper publication
that meets the statute or by posting on the municipal website); sale, by public auction or, with
council's consent, by tender. These are the steps outside Halifax under the Municipal Government
Act. See [The first clock](#sec-01-first-clock) and [Figure 1.2](#fig-01-pre-sale-timeline).

### 1.2 {#ans-01-2}

Property must be put up for sale once taxes are unpaid for the three preceding fiscal years. The
requirement is subject to the Act's exceptions and to a possible council deferral. Inverness's
two-year description is not a competing deadline: the Act sets the provincial frame, and a
municipality may operate within that window using a shorter trigger. Inverness and Chester publicly
describe accounts as eligible after two years. See [When proceedings can
begin](#sec-01-first-clock).

### 1.3 {#ans-01-3}

The line's job is to identify the property in a collection process and to tell the public where to
seek the fuller record; it is not trying to persuade anyone that Harbour Road (a fictional
composite) is desirable. A marker or parcel outline derived from an official notice helps you find
the event and the apparent parcel. It does not turn the municipality into the property's seller in
the ordinary real estate sense, and it does not turn the notice into a condition report. See
[Notice, not marketing](#sec-01-notice).

### 1.4 {#ans-01-4}

Halifax's current notice, under its own Charter framework, controls. Halifax has a parallel
tax-sale framework in the Halifax Regional Municipality Charter rather than the tax-sale division of
the Municipal Government Act, so the correct authority is different even though the broad sequence is
recognizably similar. The packet can teach you what careful municipal information looks like, and
the map can route you to the right place; neither can silently replace the authority that owns the
event. See [Halifax](#sec-01-halifax) and [Figure 1.5](#fig-01-whose-rules).

### 1.5 {#ans-01-5}

It shows that official records can disagree without either record becoming worthless (the same
packet also has lien 6 in the summary with no matching detailed sheet, and two recovery amounts
that differ between the summary and the detailed pages). It does not prove that a parcel is

### 1.6 {#ans-01-6}

The parcel shape is not a boundary survey. The aerial photograph is not a current inspection. The
line saying that no dwelling is listed does not prove that the ground is empty, safe, buildable,
serviced, or available for immediate possession. Each item can still be valuable; none quietly
answers the other questions a bidder carries into the room. See [What each part can and cannot
establish](#sec-01-packet).
