## Chapter 5 · Using the Map as a Research Tool {#ans-05}

### 5.1 {#ans-05-1}

The counts are a dated catalogue snapshot from the production build checked on July 22, 2026. They
are not a claim that the event, or every entry, remains live when you read them. The app is a
research index. The municipality's current notice or direct confirmation controls whether the sale
is live, a parcel remains listed, the event has been postponed, or a term has changed. A copied PDF,
screenshot, browser tab or map catalogue can become stale, so the direct source link is part of the
operating sequence. If the direct notice and the app disagree, the disagreement is recorded; the app
does not break the tie in its own favour. See [Current notice or historical
result](#sec-05-record-family) and [Figure 5.2](#fig-05-current-vs-historical).

### 5.2 {#ans-05-2}

Containment is a precise interface operation: this returned civic point falls inside this returned
parcel polygon. Its legal meaning remains narrow. It does not prove who owns or occupies the parcel,
whether the point is the mailing address for every interest, whether a building sits at the point,
whether the public can enter, or whether there is legal access from the road. Containment finds the
parcel to investigate; it does not finish the investigation. See [Starting from a civic
address](#sec-05-record-family).

### 5.3 {#ans-05-3}

The building count reports Nova Scotia Topographic Database point or polygon features intersecting
the mapped parcel. It does not establish how many structures currently exist, whether any are
occupied, their condition or use, or whether they have permits; a returned zero would not prove an
empty parcel either. The 2026 assessed and taxable values of $35,000 are a dated PVSC assessment.
The amount is not today's sale price, a current appraisal, a repair estimate or a bid ceiling. The
demonstration parcel is not presented as a current tax-sale listing. See [Parcel
identity](#sec-05-record-family) and [Figure 5.5](#fig-05-buildings-assessment).

dataset; a visible route may be private, unmapped, misaligned or legally irrelevant; and the parcel
geometry itself is not a survey. Visible transportation nearby does not fill the gap either:

current-notice PID used to test the interface, not a recommendation or property evaluation. See

### 5.5 {#ans-05-5}

If the note loses its limitation, the result becomes too strong: nothing says that both source
geometry and parcel geometry are screening data. If it loses the unknown and handoff, it creates
caution without progress: nothing names what remains open (legal access, ground conditions, water
constraints, current service completeness) or who can answer it (land records and a lawyer for
access; survey and current municipal or environmental sources for the physical and regulatory
questions). Source, date, observation, limitation, unknown and handoff travel together. See [A note
in six parts](#sec-05-one-question) and [Figure 5.11](#fig-05-six-part-note).

### 5.6 {#ans-05-6}

The Halifax figures answer a bounded historical question: what did the checked official records
report for that PID at that event? They do not answer what the parcel is worth now. The result is
not a comparable sale model, does not predict competition at another auction, does not establish a
discount and does not supply a bidding ratio; tax-sale outcomes occur inside their own legal,
physical, market, information and participation conditions. The CBRM record for PID `15234636`, from
the July 21, 2026 event, shows the minimum bid and notice fields, but its winning-bid field says
"Awaiting official results." It claims no sale outcome, no purchaser and no inference about present
ownership or parcel status: moving a dated notice into historical mode after its event date does not
manufacture a result the municipality has not published. See [Historical mode](#sec-05-historical).
