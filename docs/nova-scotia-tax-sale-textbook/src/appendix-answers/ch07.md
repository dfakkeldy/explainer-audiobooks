## Chapter 7 · Physical and Environmental Records {#ans-07}

### 7.1 {#ans-07-1}

The observation should remain literal: a feature is visible, or a historical record describes a
use. For the fictional Breakwater Lane, the file can say that a small building and a possible former
tank area are visible from public imagery, and record the date and source of the image. Those
observations do not establish a spill, contaminant, tank location, or present condition.
"Contamination" is not an exterior observation. See [Keep the observation
literal](#sec-07-three-records).

### 7.2 {#ans-07-2}

An empty search establishes that the researcher did not find a responsive record within the chosen
source, terms, date, and search method. It does not establish that no release occurred, that no
contaminant exists, that every historic activity was reported, or that the parcel is clean. A
registry is a collection of records created under its own rules and history, not a soil sample
spread across the province. The Breakwater Lane note's next question is to expand the address,
parcel-history, neighbouring-site, and prior-use search, then ask an environmental professional what
lawful pre-purchase assessment is warranted. See [Contaminated sites and the Environmental
Registry](#sec-07-three-records).

### 7.3 {#ans-07-3}

No. The map point is not the well. Its position may be based on the location information available
when the record was collected, and the Province warns that the database may be incomplete or
inaccurate. Containment compares two mapped objects; it does not cure uncertainty in the source
coordinates or prove that the physical feature remains where the log suggests. A log also records a
drilling event, not a guarantee of current equipment, current yield, potable water, legal
compliance, or the condition of whatever exists on site today. The current well location, parcel
association, ownership, physical condition, yield, water quality, and serviceability remain
unverified. See [Wells and well logs](#sec-07-three-records).

### 7.4 {#ans-07-4}

Nova Scotia's environmental record service warns that a record is unlikely for a property more than
seven years old, and its process information describes on-site sewage files as records with a
limited retention period. The negative result could coexist with many realities: an older system
installed under records no longer retained; a system changed, abandoned, shared, or never documented
in the searched source; a changed address or parcel relationship; a file elsewhere. So it does not
establish that no system exists, that an existing system was approved, or that any system is
suitable or functional now. Current municipal and provincial requirements, available property
history, and qualified on-site assessment remain separate next steps. See [On-site sewage
records](#sec-07-three-records).

### 7.5 {#ans-07-5}

For the coastal result: the checked Coastal Hazard Map shows scenario coverage overlapping part of
the current graphical parcel under the selected 2100 setting; this is a long-range screening
observation, not a surveyed boundary, site elevation, event prediction, engineering conclusion,
insurance determination, or permit decision. It should not say "The property will flood."

For the mine result: the checked source returned no displayed opening under the recorded query and
map state. Because the inventory is incomplete and excludes some expressions of hazard, the result
cannot support "no mining hazard"; it may still reduce one specific concern, that no known inventory
point was returned by that search. See [The Coastal Hazard Map](#sec-07-shore-and-past) and
[Abandoned mine openings](#sec-07-shore-and-past).

### 7.6 {#ans-07-6}

It is an error state: the researcher does not have a completed result. A failed service, loading
timeout, hidden layer, or incorrect selection cannot be narrated as "nothing found." The note should
preserve the failure, and the researcher should retry or use the authoritative source directly. Draw
no negative conclusion. See [Positive, negative and error](#sec-07-shore-and-past).
