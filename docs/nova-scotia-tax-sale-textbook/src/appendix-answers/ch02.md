## Chapter 2 · Reading the Notice {#ans-02}

### 2.1 {#ans-02-1}

The PID identifies the mapped parcel; the AAN identifies the assessment account. When Harbour
Road's summary row (a fictional composite) carries one AAN and one PID, the pairing is evidence that
the municipality associated a particular assessment account and parcel with the sale entry it
published. It is not proof that every building, driveway, fence, utility line or apparent yard
inside the reader's mental picture belongs to that parcel; each of those questions needs its own
source. See [Two catalogue keys](#sec-02-aan-pid) and [Figure 2.2](#fig-02-two-catalogue-keys).

### 2.2 {#ans-02-2}

It supports "The municipality published $4,000 for this dated event under its stated terms." It
does not support "Harbour Road is worth more than $4,000," and it also does not support the opposite
conclusion. The recovery amount is built around the taxes, interest and sale expenses the
municipality seeks to recover; it was not produced by inspecting the property. Without title,
access, planning, condition, occupancy, tax, repair and uncertainty work, a reader cannot derive
value merely by objecting to the small municipal number. See [The recovery
amount](#sec-02-recovery).

### 2.3 {#ans-02-3}

None of those secondary copies can settle it alone. The municipality's current notice, or direct
confirmation from the authority running the sale, says whether the lien remains in the current
event. Open the map's direct municipal source, check the current municipal notice or update, ask
the treasurer when the published sources still conflict, and record the retrieval time. The
earlier packet remains valuable evidence of what was published earlier; it does not become false
merely because the live process changed. See [A list that can shrink](#sec-02-list-shrink).

### 2.4 {#ans-02-4}

It identifies the branch without the normal six-month statutory redemption right because of the
age of the arrears: the statutes remove that right when the taxes were already more than six years
in arrears at the time of sale. It does not establish possession, insurability, condition, clean
title or permission to use the land. A map's redemption filter cannot reclassify a parcel or
interpret the statute for a live decision; the source row, the current notice and qualified legal
advice remain the route when the classification matters. See [The redemption
marker](#sec-02-redemption-marker).

### 2.5 {#ans-02-5}

One date records when the packet was retrieved; the other records the last event-status check.
Without dates, a file that mixes the original packet, a later municipal update, a map snapshot and
auction-day notes can become misleading even when every source is authentic, because the reader no
longer knows which rows existed at which point in the process. The card also needs a source beside
every amount and marker: "Recovery amount" without a page and date is a number detached from its
authority, and "Redeemable" without the current notice is a label detached from its branch. See
[Dating the file](#sec-02-dating) and [Figure 2.5](#fig-02-source-state-labels).
