## Kept hedged

1. "For most tax-sale properties" (text, summary, answer 3.2): source conditional. The older-arrears
   exception is the reason for "most" (LAW-009).
2. Intro: "the redemption that may end it, and the tax deed that may follow": real alternatives.
   Either ending can happen.
3. Careful panel: "The insurance duty contains a further uncertainty" and "may inherit a statutory
   responsibility without a practical insurance product": uncertain. INS-001's boundary says a
   specialty product is no evidence that this property will be accepted. The scope list ("vacant,
   damaged, occupied, remote…") is audit-restored (A03-01), in the summary too.
4. "Why it matters" list ("The owner may be trying to arrange funds", lender "may be assessing",
   occupant "may experience contact", contractor "may see"): these describe people whose situations
   vary from case to case. They are not hedges on the law.
5. "professional advice where required" and "the municipality's approval where the statutory
   accounting calls for it" (recall box, answer 3.3): source conditional (LAW-010: approval is
   needed for repairs).
6. "The exact facts, dates, authorization, and local administration matter in a real redemption":
   varies by case and municipality. This is the only place the chapter says it.
7. "It does not by itself prove…" (invoice): precise scope, not a softener.
8. "Those words … require legal interpretation against the actual parcel and records" (deed): a
   specific handoff to a lawyer, backed by LAW-012's boundary. It is said once in the text and once
   in answer 3.5.
9. "'Immediate' does not promise that every practical and legal question can be resolved on sale
   day": audit-restored (A03-02).
10. Relay-baton rail, "if the comparison is kept narrow", and the second-limit rail ("can mishandle",
    "can discover", "can face"): the style guide requires each analogy to keep its limit, and the
    "can"s are real possibilities.
11. "The four questions are a stage check, not a do-it-yourself legal test": the chapter's single
    method boundary. It is not a disclaimer.
12. Captions for figures 3.1 and 3.2, "Educational overview • verify current law and sale terms":
    these quote the original figures' source lines word for word, and the SVGs I may not edit still
    print them. Note for the coordinator: if those words come out of the SVG source lines, take them
    out of these two captions as well.
13. "Redeemable" "may end in discharge or deed": real alternatives.
14. The statutory "may" (powers table, deed request, "may redeem" in the figure text): these grant
    powers, so they are the rule, not hedges.
