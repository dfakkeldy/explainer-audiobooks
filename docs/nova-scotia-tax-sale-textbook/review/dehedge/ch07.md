# De-hedge — Chapter 7

Most of this chapter's "may", "can" and "does not" sentences already did the teaching work: they
say what each record does and does not establish, and many repeat the sources' own stated limits
(ENV-001 to ENV-005). Those stay. The cuts target the repeated caption disclaimer, softeners on
plain descriptions (what a well log records, what a visible pipe does to a question), and hedged
lead-ins to the composite case's notes.

## Kept hedged

- "where a fuel tank may once have stood" (case box, check question 1): this is genuinely uncertain, and the audit lists it as an original qualifier.
- "appears to show an engine-repair use" and "a possible former tank area" (File Note): these are the uncertainty being taught, and the File Notes are verbatim per the audit.
- "Its position may be based on the location information available…" and "the Province warns that the database may be incomplete or inaccurate" (text, summary, answer 7.3): this is source-conditional. ENV-002 says some positions are estimates, and the Province disclaims completeness and accuracy.
- "a record is unlikely for a property more than seven years old": this is the record service's own warning (ENV-003, audit A07-01).
- The four sewage possibilities ("An older system may have been installed…", etc.): these are genuinely open alternatives.
- "A returned approval … may identify an activity…" and "records and history may flag": these vary by record.
- "mining activity that may be poorly documented or imprecisely located": this is source-conditional (ENV-005).
- "Site conditions may change.": this is the inventory's own stated limit.
- "positions on private land can have lower confidence and may be inaccurate by roughly 50 metres" (text, Figure 7.7 caption, summary) and "can cross": these were restored by the audit (A07-02, A07-03). "Roughly" is kept per F-07.
- "It may still reduce one specific concern" (text, answer 7.5): this is an original qualifier the audit checked.
- "The uncertainty may govern the bid decision in any of three situations": this is conditional, and the audit checked it.
- "The precise legal and technical meaning belongs to the governing framework and qualified professionals" (contaminated-site definition): this is the single handoff for the definition, not a repeated disclaimer.
- File Note "None of these records replaces lawful inspection, sampling, surveying, engineering, planning, insurance, or legal advice.": this is a verbatim File Note and states the record's limit, not a legal-advice disclaimer.
- The "can reveal" sentences (well database, Coastal Hazard Map, mine inventory, environmental records): these describe what a source is capable of, not uncertainty.
