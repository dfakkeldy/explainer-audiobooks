# De-hedging pass — Chapter 8 (Title and Possession)

Read first: `review/audit/ch08.md` (A08-01 to A08-10), `review/flagged-claims.md` (F-08a to F-08c),
`review/style-guide.md` §§1–2, the original (md:945–1049) and evidence notes LAW-008, LAW-012,
OCC-001 and MOB-001.

The chapter was already fairly tight. Most of its "may" sentences are either powers ("A municipal
treasurer may apply to a court"), conditions the sources hold open, or qualifiers the audit put
back on purpose. The cuts below target caption disclaimers and softeners on points the case facts
or plain procedure already settle.

## Kept hedged

- "may continue" for the Foundry Street right-of-way in Quick recall 2, the fig-08-deed-effect
  caption and alt, and the rail. Restored by the audit (A08-01, A08-03, A08-04).
- "may define the interest or preserve an exception" (court order) in the rail, caption and alt.
  Restored by the audit (A08-03, A08-04). It only applies if an order exists.
- "possible" / "not automatically a continuing burden" for the surplus claim, and "A prior interest
  holder may have a separate statutory route". Restored by the audit (A08-04), and the source is
  conditional (LAW-014).
- "Its tax deed may discharge interests the statute discharges" (rail and answer 8.2). Which
  interests are discharged is for counsel to confirm against this file (LAW-012 boundary).
- "if an order exists" / "if one exists" / "Confirm whether this sale proceeded under a court
  order". Genuinely conditional (LAW-013).
- "A municipality may describe the statutory deed process, yet current municipal guidance also
  warns…". This is flagged as F-08a and kept as printed.
- "Other events require their own current municipal materials." The CBRM practice is CBRM's own and
  varies by municipality. The audit kept it on purpose.
- "They might include a former owner, a tenant…". The occupants really are unknown; that's the
  lesson.
- "Whether and how that state can be obtained depends on the actual facts and applicable law"
  (vacant possession). Fact-specific (OCC-001: property-specific treatment unresolved until counsel
  reviews it).
- "Those choices could affect people's rights and safety…". These are risks of self-help that
  depend on the facts.
- "The facts may lead to different processes for an owner who remains, a tenant…". This varies by
  case, and the chapter deliberately teaches no single process.
- "A room that appears unused may still contain someone's furniture…". This is a real possibility
  and is the point of the paragraph.
- "The likely cost and time of property-specific legal work may be unknown." Before a lawyer
  reviews the file, it really is unknown.
- "Land, buildings and a manufactured home may not share one simple record story" (caption), "A
  manufactured home may sit on land owned by someone else", and "A manufactured-home listing can
  involve four distinct questions" (summary). The source is conditional (MOB-001: an unaffixed home
  "can also involve" these questions), and A08-08 keeps "whether" open.
- "The book cannot settle them by naming categories that might or might not apply". This isn't a
  softener. It's the handoff to counsel, said once.
- "what easements, liens or continuing interests may matter" (fig-08-title-encumbrance-possession
  caption/alt). It matches the figure's printed question.
- Single, pointed handoffs to counsel ("Those questions belong with a Nova Scotia property lawyer
  reviewing the actual file"; "ask counsel to state the result for this property"). These are
  method, not disclaimer. The chapter has no legal-advice disclaimer to remove.
- Coat-check limit ("The analogy stops there… The ticket does not authorize self-help"). The style
  guide requires keeping the analogy's limit.
