Almost every qualifier in this chapter carries real uncertainty, which is the chapter's subject:
whether a visible track is a legal route. The "might"s at the road edge, "appears to" (the
polygon, the passage right, the file notes), "may matter" (right-of-way terms, road status, width)
and "may be permitted / may be contemplated" (zone) are what is actually unknown in the composite
case. The audit lists them as key facts, so they stay. The cuts are the caption boilerplate and
three softeners on plain points.

## Kept hedged

1. Kicker/title "The driveway that may not be a right-of-way": this is the chapter's open question,
   and it's the original's title.
2. The road-edge list ("might have been used…", "might be slightly displaced", "might cross", "could
   exist"): uncertain. The list sets out what the image can't resolve.
3. A06-01's careful panel ("…does not provide access; every other event still requires its own
   current terms and lawful access route"): audit-restored. It varies by municipality and event.
4. "Transportation may be visible nearby even when…": source conditional. It describes Chapter 5's
   empty result (MAP-005).
5. "any conditions may matter", "the key that may authorize passage", "not automatically adequate":
   these depend on the instrument (LAND-005). "Automatically" is the precise limit.
6. "A tax deed may later change the purchaser's registered interest according to the governing
   process": conditional, because a deed only follows if the sale and the redemption period run
   their course (LAW-012).
7. "Long use can raise legal questions" and "may have permission, rights tied to different land, or
   a dispute": uncertain. These are legal questions for a lawyer, as the original puts it.
8. "appears to touch / appears to cross / appears to benefit / appears to lie": the audit names
   "every 'appears'" as a key fact. The two File Notes are word for word.
9. Road-frontage list ("may permit", "may still require", "may matter" ×2) and the summary's "may
   matter": these vary with the instrument and the municipality's rules (LAND-006).
10. "A rural project may depend on on-site systems" and "A narrow parcel may have enough mapped
    area…": both are hypotheticals inside the scenario, not hedges on a rule.
11. "a zone in which the proposed use may be contemplated" and "a dwelling may be permitted" (table,
    rational-no note, Figure 6.5): the planner's answer is still unconfirmed in the case.
12. "A future researcher must still check the current rules … at the time of the decision": rules
    change over time (LAND-008). This is the only place the chapter says it.
13. "The parcel may still be useful to someone…" and "Another party may own adjoining land…": real
    possibilities that set up "their situation does not repair this file".
14. "Perhaps the access instrument is ambiguous. Perhaps the planning authority cannot confirm…":
    illustrative alternatives, not softeners.
15. "not a survey" in the captions and alt texts: the SVGs print it, and the F-06a convention covers
    it. The repeated "Parcel maps orient research; they are not boundary surveys" (§6.1) and
    "cannot certify the legal boundary" (§6.2) are two separate teaching points (access and
    building envelope), so both stay.
16. F-06c no-warranty sentence (rail, "Why it matters"): kept as printed per flagged-claims.
