Length is 87% of the original, above the brief's 60–75% aim and about level with the rewritten Chapter 1 (87%). Chapter 7's disclaimers were mostly lists of distinct record facts (what a well log holds, what the coastal colour belongs to, what an empty mine query covers). Those were turned into routing sentences that name who answers each question, so more of their words survived than in a chapter whose repetition is pure restatement. Further cuts would start removing facts listed in "What must survive".

The one remaining negative is in the careful panel ("when the intended use can't carry the remaining risk"). The source list's CBRM item was reworded and no longer counts.

The script doesn't count "no", "none" or "nothing". Fifteen sentences use one of those, and almost all of them report what a search returned ("no matching record", "returned none"), which is the chapter's subject. None of them is a disclaimer chain.

- Mine positions: "positions on private land can have lower confidence and may be inaccurate by roughly fifty metres" became "positions on private land can carry lower confidence and can be off by up to about 50 metres". "Up to" follows ENV-005 and the DP ME 10 Version 9 metadata ("up to approximately 50 metres"). See the flag below.
- Sewage: "a record is unlikely for a property more than seven years old" is kept ("a record is unlikely for a property more than seven years old"). "records with a limited retention period" became "kept for seven years and then destroyed" (ENV-003).
- Wells: "the Province warns that the database may be incomplete or inaccurate" is kept word for word. "records that can include location, geology, construction, depth, and yield information" became "Its entries can include location, geology, construction, depth and yield". - Hazard map: "A hazard map does not mean that damage will occur at every coloured place or that an uncoloured place is safe from every related hazard" became "Whether damage will occur at a particular coloured place, and whether an uncoloured place is safe from every related hazard, are questions for site work." ## Possibly wrong or unclear in the original (flagged, not silently fixed)

1. **Fifty metres.** "May be inaccurate by roughly fifty metres" reads as a typical offset. ENV-005 and the 2024 Version 9 metadata give it as a ceiling: "up to approximately 50 metres". The new text uses "up to about 50 metres". Please confirm against the metadata's positional-accuracy report.
2. **Orphaned Case B figures.** Figures 18–20 (Parcel B: serviced community, manufactured-home question card, driveway and drainage) are embedded in Chapter 7 but nothing in the chapter discusses them. They may belong to the Chapter 6 or Chapter 8 slide material. Figure 17 also calls the case "Parcel A" while the prose calls it Breakwater Lane.
3. **Contaminated-site definition is unsourced.** ENV-001 supports the registry and program statements, not the definition itself. The definition reads as the book's working definition, which is fine, but nothing in the evidence notes ties it to a statutory wording.
4. **Figure plan, `fig-search-beam`.** Its draw note labels the beam "2000-2026". No source in this chapter or the evidence notes gives a date range for the contaminated-sites register. The label should read as an illustrative search, or the range should be dropped.
5. **Figure plan, `fig-environment-records`.** The tiles mention "coastal protection rules", "mineral occurrences" and municipal records for buildings and fuel tanks, none of which Chapter 7 discusses (mineral occurrences are Chapter 5). The tiles' "who to ask next" should match the chapter's routing: environmental professional, well-log original and qualified professional, municipal and provincial sewage requirements and on-site assessment, planning and technical professionals, qualified site assessment.
