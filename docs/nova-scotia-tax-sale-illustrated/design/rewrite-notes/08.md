# Rewrite notes: Chapter 8

Original: "Title Is Not Possession" (`design/original/ch08.md`).
New: "Title, Burdens and the People on the Land" (`src/chapters/08-title-burdens-people.md`).

## Counts (`hedge_count.py`)

| | Words | Sentences | With a negative |

| Original | 2224 | 162 | 36 (22%) |

Length is 93% of the original, above the 60–75% aim. About 330 of the new words are apparatus the original did not have: the source list (about 120), the Try it rail (about 80), the careful panel (about 90) and the section headings (about 50). Without them the chapter is about 1,735 words (78%). The rest of the gap comes from keeping every fact. The original's repetition (three recap passages, the closing "No lock turns..." run, the "careful explanation" summary) is gone. What remains is case material and statute detail. For comparison, the Chapter 1 model came out at 87%.

The four negatives the counter finds, each kept on purpose:

1. A surplus claim "is not automatically a continuing burden on the land": the word "automatically" carries legal weight.
2. Current municipal guidance warns that "clear title is not guaranteed": this is the municipality's own warning.
3. CBRM "does not remove residents or provide access": this is the costly mistake for bidders.
4. "'Looks abandoned' is not a disposal standard": a mistake that costs other people their property.

The counter does not look for "no", "nobody" or "without". Counting those too, 10 of 122 sentences (8%) have a negative. Each one is part of a definition ("no other person remains entitled"), the self-help rule ("At every stage, use no self-help"), or a plain description.

## Structure and figures

- `fig-three-questions` comes after the opening paragraph that names the three questions (interest, burdens and benefits, possession). `fig-occupied-handoff` comes right after the careful panel, which introduces observing from public land and handing off to a lawyer.
- The original's figure-24 and figure-25 are replaced by those two IDs. Case B's slide figures (figure-21 planning map, figure-22 screening map) showed Chapter 7's fictional Parcel B: a former-use symbol, a registry point, a zoning, water and sewer map, and an occupancy warning. The original Chapter 8 text never discussed them, so their content is not carried here. The chapter's own cases are carried in prose instead: Foundry Street's court-order task and Harbour Park's file are `::: note` boxes.
- There is one careful panel, on occupied property. It is scoped by stage. Before the sale: observe only from a public road or other public land, and note the date and what you saw (LAND-007). At every stage: use no self-help, and take possession questions to a lawyer (with tenancy advice) before anyone makes contact with the occupants. A draft said "stay off the land" without saying when. That would have told a deed holder something the original and the evidence notes do not support, so it was scoped to the pre-sale stage. The chapter does not repeat the panel elsewhere. The original's closing "No lock turns..." run was a restatement of it and was cut.
- One Try it rail (Foundry Street's register).
- Bold: **fee simple**, **encumbrances**, **vacant possession**. Terms bolded in Chapters 1–7 are not bolded again: tax deed, certificate holder, parcel register, legal description, right-of-way, PID. See flag 6 on fee simple.

## Flags: possibly wrong or unclear in the original

1. **"Current municipal guidance also warns bidders that clear title is not guaranteed."** Neither `research/evidence-notes.md` nor `research/sources.md` names which municipality's guidance says this, or its date. I kept the sentence but could not add it to the source list. It needs a named, dated source, or it should be cut.
2. **"The tax deed is evidence that the statutory sale provisions were followed."** LAW-012 says "statutory evidence of compliance". Check MGA s. 156 for its exact word (for example, whether the Act says "conclusive" evidence) and how that sits beside the Marketable Titles Act six-year window in Chapter 12. I left the wording as it was.
3. **Tenant-property forms.** The original says the residential-tenancy system has "specific resources and forms for tenant property left behind". Sources.md item 33 cites the general tenant and landlord resources and the ending-a-tenancy guide, used "only to establish that occupied-property and abandoned-goods questions require a separate lawful process". It does not name the abandoned-goods forms. Worth confirming the forms exist under that description.
4. **Harbour Park and Harbour Road** (Chapter 1's composite) are easy to confuse. Consider renaming one of them across the book.
5. **Figure lane for mobile homes.** `fig-three-questions` puts "mobile homes" in the POSSESSION lane. This chapter treats a mobile home mostly as a question of what asset is being sold and which registry holds its records: the Personal Property Registry, land records, and the land-lease tenancy under the Residential Tenancies framework. Consider showing the Personal Property Registry in the title/burdens lane and the land lease in the possession lane.
6. **Bolding fee simple and encumbrances.** "Fee simple" and "encumbrances" first appear in original Chapter 3, unbolded. I bolded and defined them here because no earlier chapter does. If the Chapter 3 rewrite defines them, drop the bold here and use a reminder clause.
7. **"Easement"** is used in Chapters 3, 6 and 8 without a definition. Chapter 6 bolds only "right-of-way". Consider defining "easement" once in Chapter 6.
8. **"The redemption question has been resolved"** (opening) is vague. Chapter 12 says Foundry Street was not redeemed. "Redemption period ended without redemption" would be clearer, but I kept the original phrase in case Chapter 12's timeline changes.
9. Original section numbers in the source list come from the evidence notes: MGA s. 139A, ss. 146–147, s. 151, ss. 155–156; HRMC s. 154, ss. 161–162, s. 166, ss. 170–171. The `::: source` list carries only official sources. CBRM's page and FAQ cover both the occupied-property rule (OCC-001) and the rule against entering before the sale (LAND-007).
