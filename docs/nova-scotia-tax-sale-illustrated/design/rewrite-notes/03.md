Without the source list the chapter is 1,615 words (84%), and without the rail too it is about 80%. That is over the brief's 60–75% aim and close to Chapter 1's 87%. The original repeats some points, but most of its paragraphs carry a fact the brief says must survive (each certificate-holder power and its limit, each redemption-cost category, each document in the chain, the people outside the room). I kept those and cut only what was said twice. If Dan wants it closer to 75%, these are the next cuts, in order: the relay paragraph near the end, the "people outside the auction room" paragraph (it is the human frame, so I kept it), and the sentence after the four questions.

The four sentences with a negative, and why each one stays:

1. Careful panel 1: "the normal statutory redemption right does not apply" when taxes were more than six years in arrears at the sale. The brief names this one.
2. "The holder must not injure the property or cut trees". This is a statutory duty. I kept the statute's own verb because "leave the trees standing" could read as allowing limbing or partial cutting.
3. Careful panel 2: "the statute cannot force an insurer to offer a policy". The mistake costs money, and the reader inherits a duty with no product to meet it.
4. "cannot refuse it and keep the land instead". A certificate holder who has spent months on the building may think they can keep the land and refuse the repayment. Getting that wrong costs rights.

## Additions (all supported by the evidence notes)

- The treasurer **gives** the certificate to the purchaser and registers **a copy** in the land registry (LAW-007). The original said "prepares and registers".
- Interest runs **"from the sale to the redemption"** (LAW-010). The original said only "ten percent a year".
- **"and any surplus from the sale, reduce it"** (LAW-010: "adjusted for surplus"). I added this so the text matches fig-redemption-price, whose caption includes the surplus. The original chapter leaves it out. Please check this against MGA s. 152(3)–(4). The figure plan's fig-surplus-route says "If redeemed: the balance reduces the redemption amount", which matches.
- The tax deed is one the purchaser **"may request and pay a fee for once the applicable waiting period ends"** (LAW-012). The original said only "can move toward a tax deed".
- **"Get a workable insurance answer before the sale."** This comes from INS-001's boundary ("Obtain terms… before bidding"). The original implied it: "A bidder who cannot obtain a workable answer before sale may inherit…"
- **"A tax deed can also be challenged for a set period after registration, as Chapter 12 explains."** This replaces the original's "[the deed does not] guarantee that the title will never be contested". The period itself (six years from registration, LAW-018) is left to Chapter 12.

## Possible errors or unclear points in the original (not fixed silently)

1. **Is a certificate of sale only for redeemable properties?** The original says "For a redeemable property, full payment produces a certificate of sale." LAW-007 (MGA s. 150) says the treasurer gives a certificate after full payment, with no limit to redeemable files. fig-redemption-paths shows a certificate only on the redeemable branch. I kept the original wording, which is true for redeemable files either way. Someone should check whether older-arrears sales also get a certificate before the deed (MGA ss. 150, 155) and adjust the figure if they do.
2. **Redemption taxes.** The original lists only "later taxes and interest". LAW-010 also includes older taxes ("specified older and newer taxes"), as does original Chapter 11 ("certain older unpaid taxes when the purchase payment did not cover them"). The rewrite says "the taxes the statute lists, including later taxes and interest". Check this against s. 152(2).
3. **Surplus.** The original Chapter 3 leaves out the surplus adjustment that LAW-010 and the figure caption include. I added it (see Additions). Confirm the direction (it reduces the amount owed) against s. 152.
4. **Insurance risk list.** The phrase "vacant, damaged, occupied, remote, or otherwise non-standard" is the original's. INS-001 supports vacant and non-standard risks directly, and its market-practice source is rated medium confidence. "Damaged, occupied, remote" are reasonable examples of non-standard risks, but no named source states them. Kept as written.
5. **"Immediate-deed branch."** This is municipal shorthand. The evidence notes (unresolved item 4) say it should never be read as immediate possession, insurance or development. The rewrite says "describes timing only", which is consistent.
