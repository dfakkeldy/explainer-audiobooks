# About this book

Every year, Nova Scotia municipalities sell land to collect unpaid property taxes. The sales are public, the notices are published, and the rules are written down. Most people still meet a tax sale as a short line in a newspaper or a long PDF on a municipal website, with no map of how the pieces fit.

This book is that map. It follows the whole process, from the first unpaid tax bill to the deed and the years after it, and it teaches one research habit along the way: keep each record attached to its source and its date, and take each open question to the person who can answer it.

::: {.rail label="Who it's for"}
Anyone meeting a Nova Scotia tax sale for the first time: a curious neighbour, a first-time bidder, an owner or family member behind on taxes, a journalist or a student. You don't need a legal or real-estate background.
:::

::: {.rail label="How it works"}
The chapters follow a sale in order. Inverness County's August 2026 sale in Port Hood is the main real example, compared with other municipalities. Harbour Road and the other named cases are fictional composites, built to show one lesson each without pointing at anyone's property.
:::

::: {.rail label="How to read it"}
Each chapter opens with what it teaches and closes with its official sources. **Try it** boxes in the margin test one idea with a short answer. The footer of every page explains the abbreviations and new words used on that page, and the glossary at the back collects them all.
:::

::: {.careful .keep}
**This is a guide to a public process, not advice about a property.** Laws, municipal practices and sale lists change, and every sale has its own terms. Before any decision, read the municipality's current notice, check the current statute, and ask a lawyer, surveyor, planner, inspector, insurer or accountant about the questions that belong to them. This book describes how to organise those questions. It never ranks properties or suggests what to bid.
:::

## What's dated

Laws are quoted as consolidated in 2026: the Municipal Government Act to 9 April 2026 and the Halifax Regional Municipality Charter to 1 May 2026. Municipal examples carry the date they were checked, mostly July 2026. Historical sale results come from the municipalities' own published results.

## About this edition

This illustrated edition rewrites the 2026 text in plainer language and adds new diagrams and charts drawn from the official records. The facts, examples and sources are the same. The audiobook in the KinNoKi Labs Listening Room narrates the earlier text, so the wording you hear there differs from this book.

The interactive map used in Chapter 5 is NS Marks The Spot, at kinnokilabs.com/apps/nsmarksthespot/map, a free public tool by the same author.
