# Grounded evidence notes

Claim policy: `traceable-only`. Retrieved and checked 2026-07-19. The current-law source snapshots are the Nova Scotia Municipal Government Act consolidated to 2026-04-09 and the Halifax Regional Municipality Charter consolidated to 2026-05-01.

## Why a property reaches sale

### LAW-001 — tax lien priority
- Supported wording: Municipal taxes levied on real property are a first lien on that property, have priority over other claims, liens and encumbrances, and need not be registered.
- Source: Municipal Government Act (MGA) s. 133(1)–(5); Halifax Regional Municipality Charter (HRMC) s. 147(1)–(5).
- Confidence: high.

### LAW-002 — eligibility and mandatory sale threshold
- Supported wording: Proceedings may begin for taxes unpaid from the immediately preceding taxation year, but not before June 30 of the next year. Property must be put up for sale after three preceding fiscal years of arrears, subject to statutory exceptions and possible council deferral.
- Source: MGA s. 134(1)–(5); HRMC s. 148(1)–(5).
- Confidence: high.
- Boundary: A municipality may use a shorter operational trigger within the statutory permission; Inverness and Chester publicly describe two-year accounts as sale-eligible.

### LAW-003 — preliminary notice, title search and sale notice
- Supported wording: The municipality first sends a preliminary notice with at least fourteen days to pay; after that period it conducts a title search, may order a survey, and serves the owner plus mortgage, lien or charge holders with a notice giving sixty days to pay before sale.
- Source: MGA ss. 137–140; HRMC ss. 151–155; Chester tax-sale process.
- Confidence: high.

### LAW-004 — advertisement period
- Supported wording: A sale or tender must be advertised by qualifying newspaper publication or posted on the municipal website for at least thirty consecutive days; the notice can use a short property reference and direct readers to the treasurer for the full description.
- Source: MGA s. 142; HRMC s. 157.
- Confidence: high.
- Boundary: The advertisement is an identifier, not a survey, inspection report, title opinion or development approval.

## Auction and payment

### LAW-005 — auction or tender
- Supported wording: The default sale mechanism is public auction, but with council consent the treasurer may call tenders instead. Council may define an acceptable minimum when the property might not recover taxes, interest and expenses.
- Source: MGA s. 141; HRMC s. 156; Kings County tax-sale policy.
- Confidence: high.

### LAW-006 — payment clock
- Supported wording: Permitted statutory payment forms include cash, certified cheque, money order, bank draft, irrevocable letter of credit and lawyer's trust cheque; a council may authorize another legal-tender delivery method. The purchaser must immediately pay the price or at least the recovery amount, and any remaining balance is due within three business days.
- Source: MGA s. 148; HRMC s. 163, both amended in 2025; CBRM auction instructions.
- Confidence: high.
- Boundary: The exact methods a municipality will accept at a particular event must be checked in that sale's current terms.

### LAW-015 — no sufficient bidder and resale
- Supported wording: If no sufficient bid is received, the treasurer may bid the taxes, interest and expenses for the municipality. If the municipality does not buy, it may advertise again and sell later by auction for the best obtainable price or by highest tender, subject to any council-set acceptable minimum. A winning bidder who does not make the immediate payment loses that attempt to an immediate re-offer; failure to pay the balance within three business days leads to re-advertisement and resale, with resale expenses deducted from the deposit.
- Source: MGA ss. 143, 148–149; HRMC ss. 158–159, 163–164.
- Confidence: high.
- Boundary: “No bidder” does not mean the parcel becomes privately available on whatever terms an observer proposes.

### LAW-016 — people prohibited from buying
- Supported wording: A council member or municipal employee of the selling municipality, the specified village equivalents, their spouses, and a company majority-owned by one of those people may not buy the land directly or through an agent. The statute attaches a $5,000 summary-conviction penalty and possible office or employment forfeiture.
- Source: MGA s. 144; HRMC s. 159.
- Confidence: high.
- Boundary: Event registration rules may impose additional authority, identification or representation requirements; this claim lists the statutory conflict prohibition, not every bidder-eligibility rule.

### OPS-006 — postponement or adjournment is a live-event question
- Supported wording: The statutory sale notice must state the advertised date, time and place, but the reviewed MGA and HRMC tax-sale sections do not provide a general bidder-facing “adjournment” rule. A postponed, cancelled or rescheduled event must therefore be treated as a live municipal-status question and checked against the municipality's current notice rather than inferred from an old advertisement.
- Source: MGA s. 142(2A); HRMC s. 157(2A); reviewed tax-sale sections 133–157 and 147–172 respectively.
- Confidence: high for the notice requirement and for the bounded statutory review.
- Boundary: This is not an opinion that every scheduling change is valid or invalid. The governing notice and legal advice control a disputed event.

### OPS-001 — live in-person auction example
- Supported wording: CBRM's July 21, 2026 sale requires in-person registration with photo identification, uses numbered bidding cards, requires immediate payment of the minimum plus its administrative fee, and gives three business days for the balance.
- Source: CBRM Tax Sales page, lines 155–174 and 203–223 in the retrieved page.
- Confidence: high for this event; perishable.

### OPS-002 — tender example
- Supported wording: Nova Scotia municipalities can use sealed/public tenders rather than voice bidding; Kings County policy says it will endeavour to livestream the opening when it uses public tender.
- Source: MGA s. 141(2); Kings County policy FIN-05-024 s. 4.3 in 2025-02-04 Council agenda; Inverness FAQ.
- Confidence: high.

### OPS-003 — a listing can disappear
- Supported wording: A listed parcel may be paid, redeemed, removed or deferred before or around the event; a bidder should expect the live list to shrink and must re-check it.
- Source: CBRM Tax Sales FAQ and result legend; Chester process; statutory ability to stop sale when arrears are paid.
- Confidence: high.

### OPS-004 — current Inverness auction mechanics
- Supported wording: Inverness County's current August 11, 2026 sale is an in-person public auction in Port Hood. Its terms require immediate payment of the taxes, interest and expenses plus the stated $200 registration amount, with any remaining purchase price due within three business days using the accepted forms listed for the event.
- Source: Inverness Property Tax Sales page; August 11, 2026 property packet; December 2025 FAQ.
- Confidence: high for this event; perishable.

### OPS-005 — municipality practice varies within the statute
- Supported wording: Current Nova Scotia examples include Inverness and Richmond open auctions, Pictou sealed tender, Annapolis auction followed by tender treatment for unsold property, Kings's annual process, and Chester's statement that it will not hold a 2026 sale.
- Source: official municipal pages and notices collected in `municipality-comparison.md`.
- Confidence: high for the dated examples; perishable.

### OPS-007 — Halifax practice and itemized tax-sale expenses
- Supported wording: Halifax operates under the parallel Halifax Regional Municipality Charter rather than the MGA tax-sale division. Its current tax-sale page is event-specific, while Administrative Order 18 itemizes possible process charges such as advance notice, internal or external title search, survey at actual cost, notice of intention, certificate preparation, deed preparation, registration at actual cost, closing and redemption administration.
- Source: HRMC ss. 147–172; Halifax Property Tax Sales; Halifax Administrative Order 18, Schedule 1.
- Confidence: high for the legal distinction and dated fee schedule.
- Boundary: Halifax's fee categories illustrate what sale expenses can include; they are not a province-wide price list and must be refreshed before use.

### DATA-001 — minimum bid is not market value
- Supported wording: The minimum bid is built around taxes, interest and sale expenses, not a property appraisal. In CBRM's official March 10, 2026 results, 24 recorded sales had a median winning/minimum ratio of 3.17, a range of 1.00–42.25, eight sales at minimum and nine at five or more times minimum.
- Source: MGA ss. 141, 143, 148; CBRM March 10, 2026 sold-property PDF; `auction-result-analysis.md`.
- Confidence: high for the statutory meaning and this one-auction descriptive sample.
- Boundary: The sample is not a forecast or proof of value or profit.

### DATA-002 — Inverness August 2026 inventory and packet reconciliation
- Supported wording: Inverness's August 11, 2026 summary lists 45 liens, 27 marked redeemable and 18 non-redeemable, with advertised recovery amounts totalling $342,793.85 and a median of $3,412.09. The detailed portion contains 44 sheets, omitting lien 6, and differs from the summary on the amounts for liens 9 and 12.
- Source: Inverness August 11, 2026 property packet; `inverness-county-dossier.md`.
- Confidence: high descriptive extraction from the dated official packet.
- Boundary: The inconsistencies require municipal clarification; they do not establish which amount controls or a defect in any parcel.

### DATA-005 — Inverness already provides a substantial parcel packet
- Supported wording: The detailed Inverness entries already provide an account/parcel fact table, aerial imagery with a parcel overlay, a Property Online parcel map with its map-not-survey disclaimer, and the registry legal description. Some legal descriptions extend the entry beyond three pages.
- Source: Inverness August 11, 2026 property packet, representative visual review of lien-entry pages and document-wide page/text structure.
- Confidence: high for the dated packet.
- Boundary: These materials orient research but do not constitute a title opinion, survey, appraisal, inspection, planning confirmation or property recommendation.

The public results PDF contains 31 bid rows totalling $691,383.05; within those rows the median bid was 4.53 times the advertised recovery amount, 15 were at least five times and seven at least ten times.

- Confidence: high for each official measure and the reproducible 31-row calculation.

### DATA-004 — Richmond compact comparison

- Source: Richmond County Tax Sales page and June 2026 results table.

## Certificate, redemption and deed

### LAW-007 — certificate of sale
- Supported wording: After full purchase payment, the treasurer gives the purchaser a certificate of sale describing the land and price and registers a copy in the registry.
- Source: MGA s. 150; HRMC s. 165.
- Confidence: high.

### LAW-008 — interim purchaser rights and duties
- Supported wording: A certificate holder gets owner-like powers needed to protect the land, may collect rent and use the land without diminishing its value, may not cut trees or injure the premises, and must insure insurable buildings.
- Source: MGA s. 151; HRMC s. 166.
- Confidence: high.
- Boundary: These powers do not authorize trespass before purchase, unsafe self-help, development during redemption, or ignoring tenancy and possession law.

### LAW-009 — six-month redemption and immediate-deed exception
- Supported wording: The owner or another person with a qualifying interest may redeem within six months after sale, except when taxes were already in arrears for more than six years at the time of sale; those older-arrears parcels can proceed to deed without that six-month right.
- Source: MGA s. 152(1), s. 155; HRMC s. 167(1), s. 170; CBRM categories.
- Confidence: high.

### LAW-010 — redemption price
- Supported wording: Redemption generally requires the purchase sum, ten percent annual interest from sale to redemption, specified older and newer taxes, registry fee, qualifying fire-insurance premiums and treasurer-approved necessary repair costs, adjusted for surplus and purchaser income from the property.
- Source: MGA s. 152(2)–(4); HRMC s. 167(2)–(4).
- Confidence: high.
- Boundary: The purchaser should not assume an unapproved repair will be reimbursed.

### LAW-011 — purchaser repayment and end of rights
- Supported wording: On redemption the purchaser supplies requested insurance/repair amounts, is repaid under the statutory formula, and loses rights in the land when the full redemption amount is paid to the treasurer.
- Source: MGA ss. 153–154; HRMC ss. 168–169.
- Confidence: high.

### LAW-012 — tax deed effect
- Supported wording: After the applicable wait, the purchaser may request and pay the fee for a municipal deed. The deed is statutory evidence of compliance and vests fee-simple title free of encumbrances, while appurtenant and burdening easements or rights-of-way continue as the statute specifies.
- Source: MGA ss. 155–156; HRMC ss. 170–171.
- Confidence: high.
- Boundary: “Free of encumbrances” is not “free of every practical or legal problem”; easements, boundaries, possession, regulation, condition and property-specific statutory questions remain.

### LAW-013 — court-directed sale
- Supported wording: A municipal treasurer may seek a court order about arrears, interests conveyed, notice and the manner of sale; a sale under such an order has the protected effect described in the order and MGA s. 139A.
- Source: MGA s. 139A; HRMC s. 154.
- Confidence: high.

### LAW-014 — surplus proceeds
- Supported wording: Sale proceeds first pay specified municipal amounts; a balance goes to the tax-sale surplus account. A person with a prior interest may apply to the Nova Scotia Supreme Court for a proportional payment after redemption expires and before twenty years pass.
- Source: MGA ss. 146–147; HRMC ss. 161–162.
- Confidence: high.

### LAW-017 — setting aside a sale does not erase the tax lien
- Supported wording: The MGA and HRMC expressly contemplate that a tax sale can be set aside and provide that, when this happens, the municipal tax lien is not discharged.
- Source: MGA s. 133(4); HRMC s. 147(4).
- Confidence: high.
- Boundary: The provisions do not provide a do-it-yourself test for defects or a guaranteed remedy; a contested sale is a lawyer-and-court matter.

### LAW-018 — six-year tax-deed contestability window
- Supported wording: Under the Marketable Titles Act, a tax deed generally may be set aside only during the six years following its registration. After that period it is generally binding and conclusive, subject to the section's exceptions for mistakenly included land and participation by the current owner in fraud or breach of trust; the section also preserves a possible damages action for wrongful tax sale.
- Source: Marketable Titles Act s. 6(2)–(6), standalone consolidation dated 2015-09-01.
- Confidence: high for the cited consolidation.
- Boundary: The six years run from registration of the tax deed, not auction day. The exception and damages provisions require property-specific legal advice, and the section number should be refreshed against the edition used in production.

### INS-001 — statutory duty does not guarantee insurance availability
- Supported wording: The certificate holder has an insurable interest and must insure a building if it is insurable, but the municipal statutes do not compel an insurer to issue coverage. Canadian insurer materials treat vacant buildings as a special or non-standard risk for which ordinary home coverage may not continue and specialized underwriting may be needed.
- Source: MGA s. 151(c); HRMC s. 166(c); Intact vacant-home and non-standard-property pages.
- Confidence: high for the statutory duty; medium for the market-practice illustration.
- Boundary: Obtain terms for the specific parcel and certificate-holder interest before bidding. The existence of a specialty product is not evidence that this applicant or property will be accepted or that the cost is affordable.

## Identifying and researching the parcel

### LAND-001 — AAN and PID do different jobs
- Supported wording: The Assessment Account Number identifies the assessment account; the Parcel Identification Number identifies the mapped land parcel. A notice may contain both, and neither by itself proves boundaries, legal access, ownership quality or a permitted use.
- Source: Nova Scotia Deed Transfer Affidavit information; Property Online materials; Land Registration Administration Regulations definition of PID; PVSC service.
- Confidence: high.

### LAND-002 — Property Online
- Supported wording: Property Online is the provincial subscription system for land ownership, parcel mapping and related registry information. It can be searched by PID, owner, AAN and civic address; public terminal access is available at a Land Registration Office for the prescribed fee.
- Source: Nova Scotia Property Online page and user guide; Land Registration Administration Regulations s. 3(8).
- Confidence: high.

### LAND-003 — assessment is a lead, not a bid instruction
- Supported wording: PVSC assessments support municipal taxation. The assessed value, classification and account data are research leads but do not establish physical condition, legal access, auction value or a safe maximum bid.
- Source: PVSC home/search page; CBRM due-diligence disclaimer.
- Confidence: high for purpose; the valuation warning is an inference from differing functions.

### LAND-004 — map is not survey
- Supported wording: Provincial and municipal parcel maps are useful orientation tools but do not certify the precise boundary on the ground. A legal description and, when needed, a surveyor's work answer different questions.
- Source: GeoNOVA mapping products; MGA s. 139 survey provision; CBRM disclaimer that its maps are not surveys.
- Confidence: high.

### LAND-005 — legal access is separate from visual access
- Supported wording: A road visible beside a parcel, a driveway used in practice or a line on an online map does not by itself prove legal frontage or a registered right-of-way. Legal access must be established from title, plan and municipal requirements.
- Source: CBRM land-locked parcel guidance; Property Online; MGA/HRMC preservation of easements and rights-of-way.
- Confidence: high as a risk boundary.

### LAND-006 — zoning is not buildability
- Supported wording: Zoning is one input. Development can also depend on frontage, lot creation, setbacks, servicing, building/fire/by-law status, environmental constraints and permit history. CBRM offers a paid zoning confirmation and municipal-clearance process for some of these municipal records.
- Source: CBRM Tax Sales and Zoning Confirmation/Municipal Clearance pages; Nova Scotia municipal planning guide.
- Confidence: high.

### LAND-007 — exterior research only before sale
- Supported wording: The municipality does not own listed properties and cannot authorize a bidder to enter. Pre-sale observation must stay on public land or other places where the researcher has permission.
- Source: CBRM Tax Sales page and FAQ.
- Confidence: high.

### LAND-008 — Inverness now has municipality-wide planning controls
- Supported wording: Plan Inverness's Municipal Planning Strategy and Land Use By-law took effect on September 11, 2025, and Eastern District Planning Commission provides planning and permitting services. Current zoning and by-law confirmation are therefore required for an Inverness parcel rather than relying on an older assumption that rural land is unzoned.
- Source: Inverness County planning strategy and by-law notice; EDPC service statement.
- Confidence: high; refresh the map and by-law before a live decision.

### GIS-001 — public display of NS Aerial and NSPRD
- Supported wording: The Province of Nova Scotia Restricted Geographic Services License permits viewing covered information in any medium, mode or format for a lawful purpose. NS Marks The Spot publicly displays the NS Orthophotomap Database and NSPRD property-boundary services under that licence.
- Source: NS Marks The Spot `ProvinceRestrictedGeographicServicesLicense.md` and `LayerCatalog.swift`; official NS Aerial and NSPRD ArcGIS services.
- Confidence: high for public rendered display under the checked version 1.0 terms.
- Boundary: Required attribution, disclaimer, licence access, non-endorsement and exclusions must be preserved. The viewing grant is not assumed to authorize bulk raw-data or tile-cache redistribution.

### GIS-002 — required Province attribution
- Supported wording: A public rendered view from the covered Province services must carry: “Contains information obtained under license from the Province of Nova Scotia which is provided without warranty or liability for errors or omissions.” An application must link to the licence.
- Source: Province of Nova Scotia Restricted Geographic Services License version 1.0.
- Confidence: high.

## Physical and environmental uncertainty

### ENV-001 — environmental records are part of due diligence
- Supported wording: Nova Scotia's Environmental Registry can include contaminated-site notices and closure records, while the contaminated-sites program warns that soil, water and air contamination may arise from spills and historic uses.
- Source: Nova Scotia Environmental Registry and Contaminated Sites pages.
- Confidence: high.
- Boundary: Absence from an online search is not proof of clean soil, water or buildings.

### ENV-002 — well logs are clues with stated limitations
- Supported wording: The provincial well-log database includes location, geology, construction, depth and yield information, but the province disclaims completeness and accuracy; some mapped positions are estimates and a specific original log should be verified when material.
- Source: Nova Scotia Well Logs Database, user manual and georeferencing notes.
- Confidence: high.

### ENV-003 — on-site sewage records may be absent
- Supported wording: The provincial records service warns it may not hold on-site sewage records for older properties. Missing records therefore cannot be treated as evidence that a system exists or complies.
- Source: Nova Scotia Environmental Records Management System page.
- Confidence: medium; service-page statement should be refreshed when used.

### ENV-004 — coastal mapping is scenario information
- Supported wording: The Coastal Hazard Map supports address or PID searches and depicts a stated 2100 high-tide, storm-surge and sea-level-rise scenario; it is a planning clue rather than a parcel-specific engineering or insurance conclusion.
- Source: Nova Scotia Coastal Hazard Map User Guide.
- Confidence: high.

### ENV-005 — abandoned-mine inventory is incomplete
- Supported wording: Nova Scotia's abandoned-mine database is expressly incomplete and does not include surface expressions of subsidence. Its 2024 Version 9 metadata says private-land opening positions can be inaccurate by up to approximately 50 metres. A mapped point is therefore a screening record, not a parcel-specific hazard boundary or proof that no other opening exists.
- Source: Nova Scotia Abandoned Mine Openings Database description and DP ME 10, Version 9 metadata, especially the inventory limits and positional-accuracy report.
- Confidence: high.

## Occupancy, taxes and eligibility

### OCC-001 — no promise of vacant possession
- Supported wording: An occupied property may still be sold. The municipality does not remove residents or provide access, and the purchaser needs property/tenancy advice before changing possession arrangements.
- Source: CBRM Tax Sales FAQ; Nova Scotia Residential Tenancies resources.
- Confidence: high for municipal non-assistance; property-specific legal treatment is unresolved until counsel reviews it.

### TAX-001 — municipal deed transfer tax exemption for tax-sale deeds
- Supported wording: A deed given pursuant to a tax sale is exempt from municipal deed transfer tax under MGA s. 109(4). The land-registration process still requires the applicable Affidavit of Value information even when no municipal deed transfer tax is payable.
- Source: MGA s. 109(4); Inverness Property Tax Sales FAQ; Nova Scotia Municipal deed transfer and property tax service.
- Confidence: high.
- Boundary: This municipal exemption does not decide HST or the separate provincial non-resident deed transfer tax.

### TAX-002 — Nova Scotia non-resident deed transfer tax
- Supported wording: Since 2025-04-01, Nova Scotia's provincial tax on qualifying non-residents acquiring residential property is ten percent of the transferred non-resident interest times the greater of purchase price or assessed value, subject to exemptions such as qualifying individuals moving to Nova Scotia within six months.
- Source: Nova Scotia Non-resident Provincial Deed Transfer Tax page.
- Confidence: high; refresh before reliance.

### TAX-003 — HST is fact-specific
- Supported wording: CBRM tells its bidders HST applies to vacant land and commercially assessed property, but federal GST/HST rules make the treatment of real property depend on the seller, use, property and transaction. A bidder must obtain property-specific tax advice rather than apply a universal “vacant land equals HST” rule outside the event's terms.
- Source: CBRM Tax Sales FAQ; CRA Sales of Vacant Land by Individuals; CRA Real Property and the GST/HST.
- Confidence: high for the need for case-specific analysis.

### ELIG-001 — non-Canadian purchase restrictions
- Supported wording: The federal prohibition is extended to 2027-01-01 and can cover direct or indirect acquisition of qualifying residential property in specified population areas. Current regulations and CMHC guidance include exceptions, including vacant land and genuine development, and properties outside census metropolitan areas or census agglomerations fall outside the geographic scope.
- Source: Department of Finance extension notice; federal regulations s. 4; CMHC FAQ.
- Confidence: high; refresh before reliance.

### MOB-001 — manufactured/mobile homes need a separate branch
- Supported wording: Nova Scotia regulations include mobile homes in defined tax-sale provisions, but an unaffixed manufactured home can also involve personal-property security and land-lease questions. A PID-only land search is not enough for that asset type.
- Source: Selling Mobile Homes at Tax Sale Definitions and Forms Regulations; Nova Scotia Personal Property Registry guidance; Residential Tenancies land-lease guidance.
- Confidence: high.

## Unresolved conflicts and safe wording

1. HST: CBRM uses an operational shortcut (“vacant land and commercially assessed property”), while CRA guidance is transaction-specific. The book will present CBRM's current event rule and require accountant/lawyer confirmation rather than generalize it province-wide.
2. Occupancy: the tax-sale statutes give certificate holders owner-like protective and rent-collection powers, but the lawful handling of a particular owner-occupant, tenant, abandoned goods or possession dispute is not established by the auction notice. The book will not teach self-help eviction.
3. Title and federal/statutory interests: the provincial deed-effect language is broad, but a buyer should not infer the treatment of every federal Crown claim, Indigenous interest, environmental order, utility interest or property-specific statutory burden without a lawyer's title opinion.
4. “Immediate deed”: municipal shorthand means no six-month statutory redemption in the listed category. It will not be described as immediate possession, immediate insurability, or immediate development permission.
5. Inverness 2025 result count: council minutes report 35 sold, but the public results PDF contains 31 bid rows. Both facts remain visible; the book will not invent four missing results.
6. Inverness 2026 packet: the summary and detailed pages differ on two amounts and one listed lien lacks a detail sheet. The book will teach reconciliation and municipal confirmation, not choose a controlling value.
