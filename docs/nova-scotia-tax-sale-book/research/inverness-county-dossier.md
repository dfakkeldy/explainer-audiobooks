# Inverness County tax-sale dossier

Checked 2026-07-19. This is an educational research record, not a recommendation about any listed property. The live list, terms and amounts can change before the sale.

## Current municipal process

The Municipality of the County of Inverness says it aims to hold at least two tax sales each year. Accounts two years in arrears are subject to its tax-sale process. It advertises at least thirty days before the sale in the Inverness Oran and Port Hawkesbury Reporter and on the municipal website.

The current sale is an in-person public auction:

- Date and time: 2026-08-11 at 9:30 a.m.
- Place: St. Peter's Parish Hall, 260 Main Street, Port Hood.
- Winning rule: highest bid at or above the outstanding taxes, interest and sale expenses.
- Immediate payment: the recovery amount, using one of the sale's accepted payment forms, plus the stated $200 registration amount.
- Remaining balance: within three business days.
- Ordinary redeemable listing: the prior interest-holder has six months after sale to redeem under the statutory rules.
- Non-redeemable listing: the municipal marker means the ordinary six-month redemption interval does not apply; it does not promise vacant possession, access, insurability or buildability.

Primary sources: [Inverness Property Tax Sales](https://invernesscounty.ca/services/finance-taxation/tax-sales/), [2026-08-11 property packet](https://invernesscounty.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Tax-Sale_August-11.pdf), and [December 2025 FAQ](https://invernesscounty.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Property-Tax-Sales-FAQ.pdf).

### Reconciliation findings

The packet itself demonstrates why a bidder should reconcile the first-page schedule, detailed sheet, live municipal list and registry record:

1. Lien 6 appears in the summary but has no corresponding detailed property sheet in the downloaded 139-page packet.
2. Lien 9 shows $9,348.38 in the summary and $9,384.38 on its detailed sheet.
3. Lien 12 shows $57,875.01 in the summary and $57,578.01 on its detailed sheet.
4. The summary prints `N0` rather than `NO` for one redemption marker; the detailed sheet resolves it as `NO`.

These observations do not establish which value controls or imply a problem with the land. They establish a pre-bid question for the municipality and a general rule: never silently choose between inconsistent official fields.

## May 2025 auction results

The 2025 advertisement listed 50 liens. The later public results sheet contains 31 bid rows, but the municipality's June 19 minutes say 35 properties were sold and 15 were removed before sale through collection or legal advice. The four-sale difference between the results sheet and minutes is another item to preserve rather than silently reconcile.

For the 31 bid rows published in the results PDF:

Those 31 rows' advertised recovery amounts total $124,004.87. Subtracting that number from their reported bids gives $567,378.18, but that arithmetic is neither the complete 35-sale result nor the municipality's accounting definition of tax-sale surplus. Inverness Council minutes separately report that $608,693.21 in surplus was collected from the May 27 sale. The book will preserve the row count, council sale count and accounting measure rather than forcing them to agree.

## Public-book use

The public narration should use aggregate results and fictional composite parcels. It should not turn a live assessed owner, occupant or current PID into entertainment or an implied recommendation. The live Inverness packet belongs in a dated reader reference layer; the durable lesson is how to verify it.

The packet is itself a strong municipal information product: detailed entries already combine the core account/parcel table, aerial overlay, Property Online map and registry legal description. The book should acknowledge that work explicitly. Its added contribution is explaining the legal process, reconciling the packet's internal differences, layering planning and physical-risk screening, and showing where records stop and professional authority begins.

Local planning is also now a first-order input. Plan Inverness's municipality-wide Municipal Planning Strategy and Land Use By-law came into effect on 2025-09-11, with services administered through the Eastern District Planning Commission. A tax-sale research file must therefore use the current zoning map and by-law rather than an older assumption that rural land is unzoned. Source: [Inverness planning strategy and by-law notice](https://invernesscounty.ca/municipal-planning-strategy-land-use-bylaw-in-effect/).
