# Research brief

- Working title: *Beyond the Tax-Sale Packet: How Nova Scotia Municipal Auctions Really Work*
- Slug: `nova-scotia-tax-sale-property`
- Request: A long, plain-language public learning audiobook about Nova Scotia municipal tax sales: terminology, legal framework, property research, auction behaviour, purchaser responsibilities and the boundaries of a possible property-research service.
- Production mode: `governed-final`
- Listening mode: `road-book`, designed for a curious beginner listening while driving and delivering mail.
- Planned scale: deep book, approximately 40,000–45,000 words. Word count is an estimate, not a packaging floor.
- Research mode: deep, live-source research led by current statutes, government services, municipal procedures and results, with secondary public legal education used to explain risk.
- Safety classification: sensitive/high-stakes, narrowed to an educational overview and a decision framework. The book will not recommend a property, calculate a personalized bid, replace a Nova Scotia property lawyer, or promise investment returns.
- Privacy/publication: the intended finished book is public-safe. On 2026-07-19,
  the author explicitly authorized a PR containing the public-safe development
  work. This does not authorize publication of an unfinished audiobook package
  or waive the outline, pilot, manuscript, public-safety, cover, package, and
  listening gates.
- Assumed prior knowledge: beginner; familiar with ordinary property listings but not tax-sale law, title systems, municipal collection procedure, or auction operations.
- Learner outcome: understand the complete path from arrears to deed, distinguish a municipal information packet from a decision-grade evidence file, recognize risks that records cannot settle, and know how a researcher should hand questions to a lawyer, surveyor, planner, insurer, accountant, environmental professional, contractor, or tenancy specialist.
- Current-law boundary: core official sources were refreshed on 2026-07-19 and
  must be checked again before a later edition or before relying on a live-sale
  detail.
- Visual-listening target: a figure-rich Echo slideshow and video-export edition. The current plan contains 38 purposeful figures—15 QGIS map plates across three fictional/composite five-map case files, 19 diagrams/charts, two original editorial scene illustrations and two final retrieval aids. Narration must still work with the screen unavailable.

## Editorial stance

The book follows a fictional composite property through the system. It treats the assessed owner and any occupants as people, not obstacles in an investment story. It distinguishes lawful exterior observation and record research from trespass, harassment, or informal attempts to obtain possession.

The central promise is not “how to get cheap land.” It is “how to understand what is actually being sold, what remains uncertain, and what winning makes you responsible for.”

The user is not researching a personal purchase. The public edition may demonstrate his QGIS and record-research capabilities as a possible side-hustle, but it must not invent personal bidder motivation or read as an advertisement disguised as education. The recurring stance is a researcher explaining an evidence file; auction and purchase decisions remain with the reader and their professionals.

The book's value does not depend on converting readers into clients. Its primary public purpose is to demystify the process. Its brand value is indirect evidence that the author can investigate a difficult subject, reconcile imperfect records and explain the result responsibly.

## Inverness and public-companion scope update

- Keep the law, terminology and decision framework Nova Scotia-wide.
- Make Inverness County the main municipal worked example, using its August 11, 2026 packet, 2025 results and current Plan Inverness framework.
- Compare Inverness with CBRM, Kings, Chester, Pictou, Richmond and Annapolis so local procedure is not mistaken for provincial law.
- Develop a public, rights-safe QGIS companion using open-data layers plus attributed rendered NS Aerial and NSPRD service views under the Province's Restricted Geographic Services License.
- Keep current-property investment judgments and Property Online registry material whose separate reproduction terms are unconfirmed in a private working packet.
- Use aggregate statistics and fictional or completed-sale examples in public prose; do not recommend a live parcel or centre identifiable owners or occupants.
- Treat the municipality's own 139-page packet as the baseline: it already includes an account/parcel summary, aerial and Property Online mapping, and a legal description. The public companion must add reconciliation, planning, terrain, environmental/geological screening, source limits and professional handoffs rather than merely repackaging those pages.
- Keep commercial-service pricing and internal positioning analysis outside the
  public development packet.
