# Paired cover directions

Title: *Beyond the Tax-Sale Packet*

Subtitle: *How Nova Scotia Municipal Auctions Really Work*

Author: Dan Fakkeldy

Each direction will produce a coordinated 1600-by-2560 EPUB portrait and
2400-by-2400 audiobook square. Generated source art contains no text, logo,
watermark, interface or documentary claim. Typography and the KinNoKi publisher
mark are reproducible compositor layers.

## Candidate 1 — The Parcel File

**Audience promise:** careful civic explanation that turns a municipal notice
into a disciplined evidence file.

**Central metaphor:** one weathered municipal dossier opened to reveal nested
parcel-map layers, with a single amber tab marking the unresolved question.

**Composition:** near top-down institutional still life. The dossier occupies
the lower two-thirds; a calm cream title field sits above it in the portrait.
The square crop gives the file more central weight and moves the title into an
integrated upper-left field.

**Material and palette:** archival paper, blind emboss, graphite map lines and
restrained ink texture; cream, deep navy, teal and amber.

**Anti-brief:** no readable government forms, owner names, seals, money,
gavel, house keys, treasure imagery, legal-document imitation or live parcel.

**Typography:** confident condensed sans title paired with a quiet humanist sans
subtitle. Portrait line breaks: “BEYOND THE” / “TAX-SALE” / “PACKET”. The title
occupies about the upper 34 percent and aligns left. Subtitle and author sit
below the title; AUDIOBOOK is a small tracked label in the square.

**Relationship:** type feels like the dossier's editorial label while the art
shows the deeper research layers underneath it.

## Candidate 2 — The Raised Card

**Audience promise:** understand the auction without being seduced by it.

**Central metaphor:** one plain bidder card raised above a table, casting a
crisp parcel-outline shadow that stops before a magenta unknown.

**Composition:** cinematic close crop with the card rising diagonally from the
lower right. The portrait keeps a bright negative-space field at upper left;
the square uses a bold side title block without covering the card.

**Material and palette:** matte card stock, dark painted wood, directional
morning light and a trace of paper grain; pale sky, charcoal, coral-red and
electric teal. This is the bright/high-key option.

**Anti-brief:** no crowd, identifiable people, real auction venue, gavel,
currency, mansion, winning celebration, dramatic gold, real address or
documentary claim.

**Typography:** large geometric sans with asymmetric line lengths. Portrait
line breaks: “BEYOND” / “THE TAX-SALE” / “PACKET”. The title occupies the upper
left 38 percent. Subtitle runs in a narrow teal rule; author anchors the bottom.

**Relationship:** the title holds steady in clean space while the raised card
creates motion; the shadow quietly turns bidding into a research question.

## Candidate 3 — Layers of Uncertainty

**Audience promise:** follow notice → parcel → context → unknowns → handoff as a
repeatable Nova Scotia research method.

**Central metaphor:** layered cut-paper map fragments and a folded municipal
notice forming a path that ends in an intentionally open magenta seam.

**Composition:** tactile diagonal collage crossing from lower left to upper
right. The portrait title occupies a navy field woven through the paper layers;
the square wraps the title around the central open seam.

**Material and palette:** cut paper, screen-print ink, deckled edges and subtle
registration shifts; midnight blue, sea teal, warm off-white and vivid magenta.

**Anti-brief:** no literal app interface, readable parcel data, owner name,
provincial crest, pin-cloud infographic, scrapbook clutter, treasure map or
generic gradient.

**Typography:** sturdy grotesk title with a smaller bookish serif subtitle.
Portrait line breaks: “BEYOND THE” / “TAX-SALE PACKET”. Title occupies about
the middle 32 percent on the navy field; author and AUDIOBOOK remain small but
clear.

**Relationship:** type is one of the paper layers, while the open seam prevents
the cover from visually promising certainty.
