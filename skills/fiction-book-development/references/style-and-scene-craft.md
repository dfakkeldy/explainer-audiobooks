# Style and Scene Craft

Choose a prose mode by observable decisions, then let the story bend it. A style
sheet protects coherence without making every paragraph sound mechanically
identical.

## Style control panel

Record a bounded choice for each control:

| Control | Useful range |
|---|---|
| Narrative distance | intimate interiority ↔ cool observation |
| Sentence movement | clipped/pressured ↔ elastic/meditative |
| Diction | plain, idiomatic, technical, formal, archaic, regional |
| Image system | sparse/concrete ↔ recurring/symbolic |
| Description | functional/selective ↔ immersive/sensuous |
| Interior access | embodied flashes ↔ sustained reflection |
| Dialogue | direct, evasive, overlapping, mannered, laconic |
| Humour | absent, dry, affectionate, absurd, dark |
| Exposition | withheld, embedded in action, reflective, openly narrated |
| Emotional naming | mostly dramatized ↔ selectively named |

Also record:

- 3–5 positive sample sentences written for this project;
- phrase families, rhythms, and habits to avoid;
- permitted variation by viewpoint, setting, and story phase;
- profanity, violence, sexuality, dialect, and accessibility boundaries.

Do not imitate a living author. When a user names one, identify the desired craft
properties—such as compressed syntax, social observation, close psychic
distance, or tactile setting—and create an original combination.

## Prose modes

These are starting configurations, not genre cages.

### Transparent momentum

Plain-to-specific diction, close causal sequencing, selective concrete detail,
short reflection, and clean scene exits. Best when plot and character choice
should dominate the reading surface. Risk: generic competence. Counter with
precise perception and character-specific metaphor.

### Intimate psychological

Close distance, embodied thought, associative detail, unstable interpretation,
and consequences filtered through the viewpoint character's needs. Risk:
rumination without movement. Every interior passage must alter a choice,
interpretation, or emotional tactic.

### Lyrical sensory

Rhythmic syntax, controlled image systems, tactile place, and meaningful
recurrence. Risk: decorative metaphor and slowed causality. Give each image work:
reveal perception, sharpen tension, or transform a motif.

### Spare and pressurized

Compressed sentences, strategic omission, hard concrete nouns/verbs, subtextual
dialogue, and little retrospective explanation. Risk: emotional vacancy. Preserve
specific relational stakes and moments of involuntary revelation.

### Expansive social

Flexible distance, ensemble awareness, institutional or communal texture,
contrasting voices, and room for narrated context. Risk: diffuse focus. Anchor
each passage to whose fortunes change and why now.

### Comic or satiric

Precise incongruity, status friction, timing, patterned escalation, and a narrator
with a controlled angle of regard. Risk: interchangeable cleverness or contempt.
Give characters desires that remain real beneath the joke.

### Mythic or uncanny

Formal recurrence, archetypal pressure, charged objects, ritual pattern, and
selective explanation. Risk: vagueness. Keep physical action, cost, and local
sensory reality exact.

Blend at most two primary modes. Name which controls change and why.

## Scene engine

A useful scene card answers:

- Who perceives this scene, and what are they wrong or incomplete about?
- What do they want **now**?
- Who or what resists, with its own credible logic?
- What worsens if they fail or delay?
- What is unstable at entry?
- Which choice, discovery, reversal, arrival, or loss turns the scene?
- What state is different at exit?
- What pressure carries into the next scene?

A scene need not contain confrontation, but it must contain resistance and
change. Quiet scenes can turn through recognition, refusal, intimacy, shame,
commitment, or reinterpretation.

## Drafting controls

### Entry

Enter near the first meaningful imbalance. Establish viewpoint, physical
orientation, immediate want, and disturbance without front-loading explanation.
Use deliberate slow openings only when atmosphere itself creates pressure.

### Action and summary

Dramatize choices, irreversible moments, relationship shifts, discoveries, and
sensory experiences whose exact unfolding matters. Summarize repetition,
routine, travel, recovery, and low-value connective time. Expansion and
compression are editorial choices, not measures of importance alone.

### Description

Select what this viewpoint character would notice under current pressure. Let
detail reveal desire, expertise, prejudice, memory, or threat. Avoid rotating
through generic visual, auditory, olfactory, tactile, and taste inventories.

### Interiority

Thought should do work: misread, rehearse, resist, decide, associate, remember,
or revise. Avoid translating every gesture into an explicit emotion. Let readers
infer where the evidence is sufficient.

### Dialogue

Each speaker has an immediate aim, unequal knowledge, a tactic, and a limit.
Differentiate voices through attention, syntax, evasion, rhythm, and social
strategy—not phonetic spelling or catchphrases. Read dialogue without tags; if
voices collapse, repair motives and patterns before adding verbal quirks.

### Exit

End on changed pressure: a choice, cost, image with altered meaning, unanswered
action, new information, reversal, or emotional commitment. Do not append a
paragraph explaining what the scene already demonstrated.

## Read-aloud test

Read every accepted chapter aloud or use text-to-speech only as a listening aid.
Flag accidental rhyme, repeated sentence openings, breathless clause stacking,
monotonous paragraph length, unclear speakers, synthetic transitions, and places
where the prose explains its own effect. Repair locally without flattening
intentional rhythm or character voice.
