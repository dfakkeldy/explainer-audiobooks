# Learning Design

Use this reference to turn grounded research into a book a beginner can follow
in one forward-moving listen. Keep the working records in plain Markdown.

## Argument-level outline

An outline is a progression of questions and arguments, not a list of terms.
Before drafting, define:

- the listener's actual prior knowledge and durable outcome;
- the governing question that makes the journey worth taking;
- the narrative spine connecting the chapters;
- two to four throughlines that recur only when they do new work;
- varied chapter jobs such as orientation, story, mechanism, walkthrough,
  comparison, failure, application, and retrieval;
- prerequisites and promises that must be resolved before the ending;
- optional material that is useful to inspect but does not belong in the main
  listen.

For each section, write its job, argument, supporting evidence and locator,
throughline advance, landing beat, and what it must not repeat. A title such as
"Chapter 4: APIs" is not a section job.

## Chapter teaching plan

Each chapter plan states:

- **Durable outcome:** what the listener can explain or do afterwards.
- **Prerequisites:** what the chapter assumes has already become stable.
- **Definition:** the plain-English meaning of each necessary term.
- **Reason:** the problem or decision that makes the idea necessary.
- **Mechanism:** how it works at the level the listener needs.
- **Concrete case:** a real sourced example, application, or consequence.
- **Misconception:** the tempting wrong mental model to prevent.
- **Expected ability:** the observable understanding the chapter should leave.

Name the problem before introducing its term. Ground mechanisms in real people,
files, tools, commands, places, records, or systems. Use at least three distinct
beats where the material earns them, but do not force every chapter into the
same shape or length.

An explanation path normally supplies definition, reason, mechanism, concrete
case, and a useful boundary. Minor terms need only enough to support the path.
Important ideas can return after a gap to deepen, apply, compare, or correct;
bare redefinition is padding.

## Section-by-section drafting

One frontier author drafts in order. Before each section, provide:

- the complete outline;
- the relevant fact pack and source locators;
- the accepted voice direction;
- the previous section's text or a faithful summary;
- the current section's job;
- the must-not-repeat list.

Maintain a compact continuity note after each section: terms defined, examples
and analogies already used, deliberate callbacks, open promises, current
listener load, and the next section's no-repeat constraints.

## Blind sequential beginner review

Give an independent reviewer only the manuscript in listening order. Withhold
the outline, author rationale, intended abilities, and future chapters.

After each chapter, ask the reviewer to record:

- the mental model a beginner could plausibly form;
- confusions and unstable terms;
- the exact sentence or paragraph where the listener may become lost;
- any promise that seems unresolved;
- the smallest kind of repair needed.

The reviewer reports with exact locations and does not supply replacement
chapters. The frontier author accepts, rejects, or repairs each finding. After a
substantive voice or structure edit, repeat the review from the affected point
through every downstream chapter.

## Red flags

Return to the outline or chapter plan when:

- the route is a terminology inventory;
- research, outlining, drafting, and revision collapse into one call;
- a claim lacks a real source and locator;
- a section lacks previous context or a no-repeat duty;
- the chapter defines a term before giving the listener a reason to need it;
- an invented toy is the only real-world grounding;
- the reviewer receives privileged intent materials;
- the ear-pass is inferred from silent reading;
- word count is used to force filler.
