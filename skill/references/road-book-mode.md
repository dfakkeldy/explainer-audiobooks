# Road-Book Learning Mode

Road-book mode is the default for these learning audiobooks when the listening
context is not otherwise specified. It assumes the listener may be driving and
delivering mail: eyes are unavailable, attention is shared with safe work, the
audio may be interrupted, and most passages receive one forward-moving listen.

Use `focused-study` only when the listener explicitly expects to stop, rewind,
inspect figures, or work through a short lesson with full attention. Never use a
focused-study label to hide an overloaded main audiobook.

## The teaching infrastructure

A road-book is held together by curiosity and consequence, not by a terminology
inventory. Its outline records:

- a governing question the listener wants answered;
- a narrative spine that keeps the subject connected across chapters;
- history and people whose problems made the mechanism necessary;
- varied real-world applications where the mechanism changes what a system can
  do, or explains why it fails;
- deliberately varied chapter jobs such as scene, history, mechanism,
  application, comparison, failure, interpretation, and retrieval;
- memorable analogies with explicit limits.

History, people, narrative, real-world applications, and analogies are teaching
infrastructure. They are not decorative breaks wrapped around the "real"
technical material. They give an unfamiliar mechanism a reason, a setting, and a
retrievable shape.

## First-edition-plus revision

When an earlier edition produced real learning or curiosity, use
`first-edition-plus`. Record the source edition and preserve its governing
question, narrative spine, successful examples, and varied chapter jobs. Repair
specific weaknesses and deepen selected mechanisms without replacing the proven
learning architecture with a new syllabus.

Use `new-book` only when there is no successful earlier learning structure to
preserve. A disliked phrase, one buried definition, or an incomplete mechanism
does not by itself justify a ground-up curriculum replacement.

## What deeper means

For a road-book, deeper means more consequences, comparisons, worked
applications, failure cases, and return visits to a small set of useful ideas.
It does not automatically mean more vocabulary, lower-level mathematics, more
architecture variants, or a wider specialist taxonomy.

Put derivations, variant catalogs, metric catalogs, and specialist terminology
in optional study material: an EPUB figure, non-narrated appendix, study note, or
separate short focused lesson. The main listen must remain complete without that
layer.

## Concept budget

A beginner road-book chooses six to ten durable learner outcomes for the whole
book. Each chapter normally introduces no more than two or three genuinely new
core terms. A term is core only when the listener needs it to achieve one of the
durable outcomes; useful but nonessential specialist names belong in optional
study material.

Record each new term with its **problem before name**: the listener-visible
situation, failure, or observation that makes the label useful. Naming and
defining a term beside a toy example does not satisfy this rule. The need must be
felt before the term arrives.

## Audio working-memory budget

Main-listen calculations are intuition aids, not spoken worksheets. A brief
spoken calculation may carry at most three temporary values. Keep a symbolic chain
to at most three steps, followed immediately by a concrete reset. If the explanation needs
visual persistence, backtracking, a long sequence of operators, or several
nested distinctions, move it to optional study material.

A `focused-lesson` exception is one to five minutes, explicitly labelled, and
still limited to five temporary values and five symbolic steps. It is not a way
to place a derivation-heavy chapter inside a road-book.

Dense terminology has the same cost as arithmetic. When several unfamiliar
terms must be held simultaneously before any of them does useful work, reduce
the set or move the specialist layer out of the main listen.

## Real-world grounding

Every core mechanism needs at least one actual application or consequence.
Book-level grounding must vary: repeating one invented classroom toy does not
replace postal recognition, machine translation, cheque processing, field work,
product behavior, or other real systems with distinct consequences.

An invented throughline may connect chapters, but it cannot be the book's only
evidence that a mechanism matters.

## Analogy contract

For every core concept, record either an analogy or why an analogy would mislead.
A usable analogy records:

1. the relationship it explains;
2. at least two explicit correspondences between the analogy and the mechanism;
3. where the analogy stops matching.

The analogy must compress a relationship the listener can reuse. A colorful
comparison that supplies mood but no correspondence is decoration, not learning
evidence.

## Retrieval after a gap

Presence is not learning. Plan a retrieval after a chapter gap in a fresh
situation, let the listener recognize or explain the idea, then supply the
answer naturally. Record the listener task and where the answer appears.

One isolated definition can be missed. One enormous dedicated chapter can also
fail. Durable vocabulary is built through small, purposeful returns.

## Blind sequential beginner review

The beginner reviewer receives only the manuscript in listening order, up to the
current chapter. Withhold the outline, coverage ledger, expected abilities, and
author rationale. After every chapter, the reviewer records:

- the mental model a beginner could plausibly form;
- confusions and unstable terms;
- the exact point where the listener may become lost.

The reviewer does not grade whether the manuscript resembles the plan. The
review tests what survives one sequential listen without privileged knowledge of
the author's intent.

## Narrated comprehension pilot

Before full drafting, render a representative 10 to 15 minute narrated pilot.
It includes the opening orientation, the first technical passage, no more than
three durable terms, at least two real applications or consequences, and one
retrieval in a fresh example.

The intended listener hears the pilot in a representative context whenever
safe and practical. Surface one lightweight choice—`continue` or `revise`—and
accept optional listener notes without requiring written comprehension answers.
Record the exact pilot audio hash, listening context, decision, optional notes,
and evidence in `research/comprehension-pilot.json`. The decision must be
recorded before full drafting. Outline approval, a polished text sample, or an
agent review cannot substitute for this gate.

## Human comprehension authority

The human listener is the acceptance authority for learning. A learning receipt
proves that the required records, budgets, reviews, hashes, and accepted pilot
exist. It does not certify learning transfer. A later negative listening verdict
overrides the receipt, prose score, package receipt, audio verification, and
prior outline approval.

Stop production when the listener says the book does not teach. Preserve the
failed artifact as evidence when appropriate; do not reinterpret the verdict as
a request for more terminology or spoken mathematics.

## Word count

Word count is a planning estimate, not a packaging floor. Preserve the original
estimate and require approval for a scope reduction after drafting starts, but
do not add exposition merely to reach the estimate. When the promised learning
outcomes are complete, the chapter or book may finish below the estimated range
and the receipt records the variance without failing.
