# Road-Book Learning Mode

Road-book mode is the default when the listening context is not otherwise
specified. It assumes the listener may be driving and delivering mail: eyes are
unavailable, attention is shared with safe work, the audio may be interrupted,
and most passages receive one forward-moving listen.

Use `focused-study` only when the listener expects to stop, rewind, inspect
figures, or work through a short lesson with full attention. Never use that
label to hide an overloaded main audiobook.

## Teaching infrastructure

A road-book is held together by curiosity and consequence, not by a terminology
inventory. Its outline uses:

- a governing question;
- a narrative spine;
- history and people whose problems made the mechanism necessary;
- varied real-world applications and consequences;
- deliberately different chapter jobs;
- memorable analogies with explicit limits.

These are teaching infrastructure, not decorative breaks. They give an
unfamiliar mechanism a reason, setting, and retrievable shape.

## What deeper means

Deeper means more consequences, comparisons, worked applications, failure
cases, and return visits to a small set of useful ideas. It does not
automatically mean more vocabulary, lower-level mathematics, architecture
variants, or specialist taxonomy.

Put derivations, catalogs, and specialist terminology in optional material: an
EPUB figure, non-narrated appendix, study note, or separate short focused
lesson. The main listen must remain complete without that layer.

## Concept budget

Choose six to ten durable outcomes for a beginner book. A chapter normally
introduces no more than two or three genuinely new core terms. A term is core
only when the listener needs it to achieve an outcome.

Give the listener the **problem before name**: the situation, failure, or
observation that makes the label useful. A definition beside a toy example does
not satisfy this rule.

## Audio working-memory budget

A brief spoken calculation may carry at most three temporary values and three
symbolic steps, followed immediately by a concrete reset. If the explanation
needs visual persistence, backtracking, a long operator chain, or nested
distinctions, move it to optional material.

A short focused lesson may use up to five temporary values and five symbolic
steps, but it must be clearly bounded. Dense terminology has the same cost as
arithmetic: reduce simultaneous unfamiliar terms until each does useful work.

## Real-world grounding and analogy

Every core mechanism needs a real application or consequence. Book-level
grounding must vary; one invented classroom toy cannot carry the whole subject.

For an analogy, state the relationship it explains, at least two
correspondences, and where the comparison stops matching. Mood without
correspondence is decoration.

## Retrieval and optional study

Return to important ideas after a gap in a fresh situation. Let the listener
recognize or explain the idea before supplying the answer naturally.

Word count is a planning estimate, never a packaging floor. End a chapter when
its promised learning job is complete. Do not add exposition merely to reach a
number.
