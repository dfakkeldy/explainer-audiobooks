# Development notes

## Book brief

This public guide helps an adult in Inverness County with work and life experience but no university degree identify and pursue the best realistic next job available to them. “Best” means the strongest whole-life fit across usable income, hours, schedule, transport, caregiving, physical and emotional demands, benefits, stability, respect, growth and qualification gap. It does not mean the first vacancy, the highest title or the claim that formal education is unnecessary for every career.

The primary reader may be changing jobs, returning after time away, working seasonally, using inconsistent internet, sharing transport, managing care, or feeling uncomfortable with formal applications. The draft treats these as design conditions rather than deficiencies.

## Current-source research plan

Version 0 uses a compact source hierarchy:

1. official Nova Scotia and Government of Canada sources for employment services, labour-market information, education, apprenticeship, adult learning, employment standards and scam prevention;
2. local institutional and service sources for NSCC Strait Area Campus, municipal employment, regional transit, library and regional job-search access;
3. current local or regional listings only as dated examples of how to read requirements, hours, status and risks;
4. practical editorial advice that is explicitly not presented as a sourced fact.

For the final edition, re-open every source, record the check date, archive or quote only the minimum needed to support the claim, and replace or remove anything that no longer matches. Add local interview evidence only with consent and a recorded privacy choice.

## Risk and currency plan

### Perishability classes

- **Check at publication and point of use:** live vacancies, wages, application deadlines, minimum wage, transit fares and booking rules, course intakes, funding, service hours and contact details.
- **Check at publication:** program descriptions, apprenticeship and prior-learning processes, Labour Standards scope, scam-reporting routes and occupational outlook periods.
- **Relatively durable but still review:** resume method, evidence storytelling, job-quality comparison, application tracking and interview preparation.

### Claim rules

- Keep an explicit date on every listing and wage example.
- Use examples to teach a reading method, never to promise an employer, wage or vacancy.
- Keep province-wide labour data separate from local openings.
- Link to the original source, not a copied listing, where possible.
- Do not infer employer culture or hiring flexibility from a posting.
- Do not turn “up to,” “asset,” “may,” “equivalent” or an outlook rating into a guarantee.
- Remove a perishable detail when it does not materially teach the method.

### Release gate

No public release package should be labelled final until every perishable source has a current check date and the source note, manuscript and worksheet agree.

## Visual and cover direction

The design should feel warm, capable and editorial rather than institutional or motivational. Use a bright or high-key base, strong readable typography, generous space and a limited palette grounded in working landscapes: warm paper, deep Atlantic blue or charcoal, field green, rust or safety orange as a restrained accent.

Maps should show decision boundaries—local, commutable and remote—rather than tourist attractions. Pathway diagrams should show evidence, requirements, bridges and job quality without implying a single ladder. Annotated resumes, posting excerpts, weekly calendars and scorecards should be readable at phone and print sizes.

Photography, if licensed and consented, should show tools, hands, work surfaces, roads, community buildings, weather and ordinary workplaces without turning residents into scenery. Avoid generic corporate smiles, handshake images, graduation caps, heroic lone figures, empty scenic postcards and imagery that romanticizes hardship.

The eventual cover should use one clear human-scale metaphor for choosing a workable path from a real place. It should remain legible as a thumbnail, avoid invented local geography, and leave room for the long title and subtitle. In accordance with the workflow, three distinct cover candidates will be developed only after the author’s manuscript changes, followed by explicit selection or a requested mix.

## Publication recommendation

Proceed as a **public book with a community-partnered review stage**. Do not retreat to a private prototype: the draft is public-safe and the subject benefits from broad access. Do not present version 0 as authoritative local guidance either. A small prepublication circle of job seekers, employment-service or library staff, employers or supervisors from different sectors, and education or apprenticeship contacts should test its accuracy, tone and usefulness before the final public edition.

Community partnership should improve evidence and distribution without giving any single employer, agency or institution editorial control. Contributions need consent, attribution and privacy choices. A partner logo or endorsement should be used only under an explicit agreement.
