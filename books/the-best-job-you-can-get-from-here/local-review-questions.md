# Questions for later local interviews and lived-experience review

These are intentionally unanswered in v0. No anecdote should be invented to cover the gap.

## Job seekers

- Which parts of a formal application feel most discouraging or confusing locally?
- How do people without reliable cars define a workable search radius in practice?
- Which seasonal transitions are hardest, and which experience carries over better than people expect?
- What makes a job feel meaningfully better besides the hourly rate?
- How do caregiving schedules affect availability, follow-up and interview travel?
- What wording about education, gaps and confidence feels respectful rather than patronizing?
- Where do people actually hear about openings before or outside a public posting?
- What costs—clothing, screening, fuel, certification, tools, child care—most often block a promising application?

## Employers and supervisors

- Which entry requirements are truly non-negotiable and which are commonly written more strongly than intended?
- What evidence makes an experienced applicant without a degree credible?
- Which short credentials actually change hiring decisions?
- Which resume or interview mistakes are easy to fix?
- What does reliable performance mean in different sectors, beyond simply “showing up”?
- How should a candidate ask about schedule, training, travel and advancement without being read as uncommitted?

## Employment-service, education and community partners

- Which services can readers access in Inverness and the Strait now, and what requires an appointment?
- What low-bandwidth, phone-based, printing or computer-access options are dependable?
- Which current programs have wait lists, eligibility rules, costs or funding conditions that a short description could hide?
- Which local credentials are repeatedly requested in postings, and which are often purchased without leading to a clear opportunity?
- Where should a reader seek help if literacy, disability accommodation, language, criminal-record history or identity documents affect the search?

## Small-community dynamics

- How do people ask for discreet leads while still employed?
- How much do informal reputation and family connection matter across different parts of the county?
- What helps a person rebuild confidence or references after a difficult workplace, long absence or move home?
- Are there important Acadian, Mi’kmaw, Gaelic, newcomer, disability or other community perspectives the draft fails to represent?

## Review standard

Interview material should be used only with clear consent, an agreed level of identification, and a chance for the contributor to check sensitive quotations or personal details. Composite characters should not be presented as real people. Anonymous examples should remove identifying details without changing the substantive lesson.
