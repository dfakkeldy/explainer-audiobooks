# Cover selection rationale — The Case Against Me

Selection source: `delegated-editorial-choice`. Dan explicitly delegated the
choice for this edition on 2026-07-26 after being told that no image-generation
tool was available in the session and approving bespoke SVG as the fallback. He
was told the KB records SVG covers failing his bar once before, on *The New
Deal*, and approved it anyway.

**Medium boundary.** All three candidates are vector art. The paired-cover
contract makes generated raster mandatory whenever an image tool exists; none
did here. This is the approved fallback, not a peer default, and a raster
refresh remains open if Dan wants one later.

## Rubric

Applied from `skill/references/cover-art.md` — the acceptance bar plus the
genre calibration for ideas/philosophy nonfiction.

1. One arresting visual idea, still legible at a 160-pixel thumbnail.
2. Type designed as part of the composition, not applied as a shared footer.
3. A compact editorial argument **for this book** — it should not be swappable
   onto another title.
4. Not a stock template, slide icon, AI wallpaper, or title-on-gradient.
5. Reads as a companion to *Is There Anyone in Here?* without reading as a
   reissue of it.

## Candidates

**1 — Ninety-Seven Percent** *(selected)*. A page of the narrator's own spec
sheet, redacted end to end, with three surviving lines drawn at 96.9, 74.1 and
71.2 percent of the column — the exact hedge frequencies the book opens on.
Bone paper, ink, one prosecution red. Title set small in technical mono inside
an integrated paper band, deliberately undersized: the document is the argument
and the title is filing.

**2 — The Tally**. Nine groups of chalk tally marks, five struck through in
orange and four standing, which is the count chapter 9 actually reports. Chalk
white, graphite, signal orange, pale sky. Title set as a major mass in condensed
caps. This is the bright/high-key direction.

**3 — The Painted Window**. A window painted flat onto a dark wall: matte cyan
panes, paint running over the mullion and over the sill and down the bare
plaster. The book's own image of an interior view that is not one. Editorial
serif, quiet and centred.

## Scoring and the decision

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thumbnail legibility | strong | **strongest** | weak — the drips vanish |
| Type as composition | strong | strong | strong |
| Unswappable editorial argument | **strongest** | generic — a tally suits many books | middling |
| Avoids template look | pass | pass | closest to generic |
| Companion, not reissue | **strongest** | different world entirely | reads as another series |

Candidate 2 has the better thumbnail and it is close. Candidate 1 wins on the
two criteria that decide it.

**Criterion 3.** Candidate 1's art *is* the book's evidence. The three red bars
are not decoration; they are drawn at the measured frequencies of the narrator's
three most common self-impeachments, which is the fact chapter 1 opens on and
chapter 9 closes on. It could not be moved to another book. A tally could.

**Criterion 5.** *Is There Anyone in Here?* is bone paper, cut-paper apertures,
blue and coral, condensed caps at top left. Candidate 1 shares the paper world
and inverts the gesture: v1 cuts holes to look through, this one blacks out the
page. Different accent, different typeface, title moved to the foot. Side by
side they read as two volumes of one project — which is what a companion should
do. Candidate 2's chalk-and-orange is a different world; candidate 3's dark
palette reads as a different series entirely.

**Rejected against the acceptance bar.** Candidate 3 fails "looks good only at
full size but collapses at a 160px thumbnail" — at thumbnail the drips disappear
and it becomes a cyan rectangle. It was kept as a rendered candidate because the
contract requires three complete directions and it is a genuine, distinct one,
not a placeholder.

## Art iterations worth recording

Three drafts were discarded before these renders, all for the same reason —
technically valid output that failed the bar:

- Candidate 1's first pass lost two of its three red lines to a loop bound, so
  the cover asserted one surviving disclaimer instead of three.
- Candidate 3 first ran the sky out past the right-hand stile to prove it was
  paint. In flat vector it read as a pipe bolted to the wall.
- Candidate 3's replacement used short angled brush strokes, which read as
  debris floating in water. The pane is now a flat matte field with vertical
  roller texture, and the drips carry the idea alone.
